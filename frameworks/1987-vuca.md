# VUCA

**Stands for volatility, uncertainty, complexity and ambiguity. It names four separate ways a situation can be hard, though people often use it as a single buzzword.**

1987 · US Army War College, Warren Bennis and Burt Nanus · [Key paper](https://doi.org/10.1108/02621719210018208) · Feeds 1 axis

## The idea

VUCA stands for volatility, uncertainty, complexity and ambiguity. The US Army War College began using it in the late 1980s, drawing on Warren Bennis and Burt Nanus's writing on leadership. As the Cold War ended, officers needed a way to say that the old and predictable enemy was gone.

The four words name four different ways a situation can be hard. Volatility means things change fast and often, but you understand what is changing. Uncertainty means you know roughly what could happen but not which of those things will. Complexity means there are so many linked parts that you can't trace the effects of any one move. Ambiguity means you don't even know what the facts mean, because nothing like this has happened before. The trait this sorts on is which of the four kinds of hard a situation has.

## What goes wrong

The common mistake is to say "it's a VUCA world" and stop there. Used as one buzzword, the label hides which of the four you face, so you can't pick the right response.

## What to do instead

Each one calls for a different response. In 2014 Nathan Bennett and James Lemoine set this out in the Harvard Business Review. Volatility calls for slack, like spare stock or cash. Uncertainty calls for more information. Complexity calls for people who know the parts well. Ambiguity calls for small trials to see what works.

## Examples

### Oil in April 2020

On 20 April 2020 a US oil futures contract fell below zero for the first time, settling at about minus $37 a barrel. This was volatility. The cause was clear, as lockdowns had crushed demand and storage tanks were full. Firms with spare cash and spare storage rode it out. More information would not have helped, because everyone knew why the price moved.

### The Ever Given

In March 2021 a container ship got stuck sideways in the Suez Canal for six days. Hundreds of ships queued and goods arrived late around the world. This was complexity. Each link in the supply chain was simple on its own, but nobody could trace how one blocked canal would ripple through thousands of firms.

### Brexit

After the June 2016 vote, firms could list the possible outcomes, from a close trade deal to no deal at all. They didn't know which one would come. That was uncertainty. The right response was to gather information and plan for each case, rather than wait for things to settle.

- [Developing strategic leadership: the US Army War College experience](https://doi.org/10.1108/02621719210018208), Herbert Barber, *Journal of Management Development*, 1992. An early account of how the War College built strategic leadership into its teaching, starting from the world its leaders would face.
- [VUCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VUCA) on Wikipedia, which traces the term from the War College into business.
- [What VUCA really means for you](https://hbr.org/2014/01/what-vuca-really-means-for-you), Nathan Bennett and G. James Lemoine, *Harvard Business Review*, 2014. The short piece that matches each of the four words to its own response.
- [What a difference a word makes: understanding threats to performance in a VUCA world](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bushor.2014.01.001), Nathan Bennett and G. James Lemoine, *Business Horizons*, 2014. A longer academic paper by the same authors on how each of the four threatens performance.
