# VUCA

VUCA stands for volatility, uncertainty, complexity and ambiguity. The US Army War College began using it in the late 1980s, drawing on Warren Bennis and Burt Nanus's writing on leadership. As the Cold War ended, officers needed a way to say that the old and predictable enemy was gone.

The four words name four different ways a situation can be hard. Volatility means things change fast and often, but you understand what is changing. Uncertainty means you know roughly what could happen but not which of those things will. Complexity means there are so many linked parts that you can't trace the effects of any one move. Ambiguity means you don't even know what the facts mean, because nothing like this has happened before.

Each one calls for a different response. In 2014 Nathan Bennett and James Lemoine set this out in the Harvard Business Review. Volatility calls for slack, like spare stock or cash. Uncertainty calls for more information. Complexity calls for people who know the parts well. Ambiguity calls for small trials to see what works. The common mistake is to say "it's a VUCA world" and stop there. Used as one buzzword, the label hides which of the four you face, so you can't pick the right response.

## Examples

**Oil in April 2020.** On 20 April 2020 a US oil futures contract fell below zero for the first time, settling at about minus $37 a barrel. This was volatility. The cause was clear, as lockdowns had crushed demand and storage tanks were full. Firms with spare cash and spare storage rode it out. More information would not have helped, because everyone knew why the price moved.

**The Ever Given.** In March 2021 a container ship got stuck sideways in the Suez Canal for six days. Hundreds of ships queued and goods arrived late around the world. This was complexity. Each link in the supply chain was simple on its own, but nobody could trace how one blocked canal would ripple through thousands of firms.

**Brexit.** After the June 2016 vote, firms could list the possible outcomes, from a close trade deal to no deal at all. They didn't know which one would come. That was uncertainty. The right response was to gather information and plan for each case, rather than wait for things to settle.
