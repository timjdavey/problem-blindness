# High ground vs swampy lowlands

**On the high ground problems are tidy and theory solves them. In the swamp they are messy and confusing, and they are often the ones that matter most.**

1983 · Donald Schön · [Original book](https://books.google.com/books/about/Reflective_Practitioner.html?id=oYNHAAAAMAAJ) · Feeds 1 axis

## The idea

Donald Schön, an American philosopher who studied how professionals think, pictured their work as a landscape. On the high ground the problems are clear enough for research and technique to solve. You know the goal, the facts are in and there is a method that works. Down in the swamp the situation is a confusing mix of problems that won't hold still long enough for a textbook answer. The trait Schön sorts on is how well a situation fits the theory a professional was trained in.

His sharpest point is about what matters. The tidy problems on the high ground are often less important to people's lives, however interesting they are to experts. The problems in the swamp are the ones people care about most. So a professional faces a choice. They can stay on the high ground and work on what their method can handle, or go down into the swamp and give up some rigour for relevance.

## What goes wrong

Schön thought many professionals quietly choose the high ground. They recast a messy situation as one their method fits, then solve that version well. The trouble is that the version they solved is not the one that was there.

## What to do instead

Schön's answer was what he called reflection in action. A good practitioner treats each case as partly new. They try a way of framing it, watch how the situation responds and change the framing when it doesn't fit.

## Examples

### Pruitt-Igoe

In 1954 St Louis opened Pruitt-Igoe, a public housing estate of 33 blocks, each eleven storeys tall. It answered the high ground question of how to house many people cheaply on cleared land. Within two decades it was badly run down, and demolition began in 1972. Historians now point to falling funds, racial segregation and the loss of city jobs as much as to the design. None of those were questions the planners had been asked to answer.

### The tired patient

A patient tells their GP they feel tired all the time. The high ground route is a blood test for anaemia or a thyroid problem, which is easy to order and easy to read. Often the tests come back normal. The real cause sits in the swamp, in things like poor sleep, money worries or grief. A GP who only treats what the tests can measure will do that job well and still miss the patient.
