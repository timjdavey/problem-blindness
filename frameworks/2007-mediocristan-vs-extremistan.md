# Mediocristan vs Extremistan

**In Mediocristan no single case moves the total much, like human height. In Extremistan one case can outweigh all the rest, like wealth.**

2007 · Nassim Nicholas Taleb · [Original book](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/56380/the-black-swan-by-taleb-nassim-nicholas/9780141906201) · Feeds 1 axis

## The idea

Pick a thousand people at random and weigh them. Now add the heaviest person on earth to the group. The average barely moves, because even a very heavy person is only a few times heavier than normal. Now do the same with wealth and add the richest person on earth. That one person now holds almost all the group's money, and the average tells you nothing about anyone in it.

Taleb named these two worlds in The Black Swan. In Mediocristan no single case can move the total much. Height, weight and calories eaten live here. In Extremistan a single case can outweigh all the others put together. Wealth and book sales live here, and so do city sizes and market crashes. The sorting trait is whether one case can outweigh all the rest. Statisticians call the second kind fat-tailed, because the rare events at the edges are far bigger and more common than a bell curve predicts.

## What goes wrong

The tools that work in Mediocristan are averages, spreads and the past as a guide to the future. In Extremistan they break. The past can look calm for years because the event that matters most hasn't happened yet. The mistake is to measure an Extremistan problem with Mediocristan tools and then feel safe because the numbers look normal.

## What to do instead

The better response is to ask whether one case could outweigh all the rest. If it could, plan for the extreme rather than the average, and avoid bets that one bad day could ruin.

## Examples

### Black Monday

On 19 October 1987 the Dow Jones fell by 22.6% in a single day. Models that treated daily price moves as a bell curve said a fall that size was so unlikely it should never happen at all. Markets live in Extremistan, so the models were wrong about the very days that mattered most.

### Book sales

A publisher who plans next year on its average sales per book is planning for a book that rarely exists. Most titles sell modestly, while a tiny number sell millions. Harry Potter turned Bloomsbury from a small London publisher into a much larger firm. The average hides the fact that one book can make or break the year.

### Your commute vs your pension

Your journey to work takes about the same time each day, give or take a few minutes. It is safe to plan on the average. Stock markets fell by about a third in 2008, and your pension went with it. There the average year is a poor guide to the one that hurts you.
