# Simple, complicated and complex

Baking a cake, landing on the moon and bringing up a child are all hard in their own way. But they are hard for different reasons. In 2002 Sholom Glouberman and Brenda Zimmerman used these three tasks to show the difference. They wrote it in a paper for a Canadian commission on the future of health care.

A simple problem has a recipe. Follow the steps and you get roughly the same result each time, without needing to be an expert. A complicated problem needs teams of experts and careful planning, but once you have solved it you can do it again. The parts behave the same way each time. A complex problem is different in kind. The parts adapt and react to each other, so what worked last time is no guide to next time.

Two traits split them. Needing experts separates simple from complicated, and whether success can be copied separates complicated from complex. Their point was aimed at health reform. Planners treated the health system as a complicated machine that a good enough blueprint could fix. Glouberman and Zimmerman argued it was complex, full of patients and staff who adapt to whatever you do. The mistake is to bring a rocket scientist's plan to a problem that behaves like a child.

## Examples

**The cake.** A recipe tells you how much flour and how long to bake. A beginner can follow it and get a decent cake. Expertise helps, but the recipe does most of the work.

**Apollo 11.** In July 1969 NASA landed two men on the moon. It took hundreds of thousands of people and many years of work. Once it worked, NASA did it again five more times, because rockets and orbits behave the same way each time. That is complicated, not complex.

**The NHS computer system.** In 2002 the English NHS launched a national programme to move patient records onto one central computer system. It was planned like a rocket launch, with a central design and huge contracts. Doctors and hospitals each worked in their own way and resisted a system built without them. The programme was broken up in 2011 after costing around £10 billion.
