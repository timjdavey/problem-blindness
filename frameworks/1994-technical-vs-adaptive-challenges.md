# Technical vs adaptive challenges

**A technical problem can be solved with known expertise. An adaptive challenge needs the people involved to change their values, habits or beliefs.**

1994 · Ronald Heifetz · [Original book](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1090937M/Leadership_without_easy_answers) · Feeds 2 axes

## The idea

Some problems have a known fix and someone who knows how to apply it. You bring the problem to the expert, they do the work and you go home. Other problems only go away when the people who have them change how they think or live. Nobody can do that part for them, however skilled they are.

Ronald Heifetz, who trained as a doctor before teaching leadership at Harvard, called the first kind technical and the second kind adaptive. He set this out in *Leadership Without Easy Answers* in 1994. The trait that splits them is where the work sits. In a technical problem the work sits with the expert. In an adaptive one it sits with the people facing the problem, and it often means giving up something they value.

## What goes wrong

Heifetz argued that the most common failure of leadership is treating an adaptive challenge as a technical one. It is tempting because technical fixes feel good. They are quick and visible, and they let everyone avoid the painful change. The problem comes back, and each time people grow more sure that someone else should have fixed it.

## Examples

### The heart patient

A surgeon can unblock a man's arteries in a few hours. That part is technical. Whether he lives another twenty years depends on whether he stops smoking, eats differently and exercises. No surgeon can do that for him, and a patient who thinks the operation was the cure is likely to be back.

### Kodak

Kodak's fall is told in full under [Single vs double-loop learning](1978-single-vs-double-loop-learning.md). In Heifetz's terms its problem was never technical, since its own engineers built one of the first digital cameras in 1975. The hard part was adaptive. A firm built on the high margins of film had to accept a world where few people bought prints, and it never did.

### The late projects

A team keeps missing deadlines, so the manager buys new project tracking software. The deadlines keep slipping. The real cause is that nobody will tell the boss a date is unrealistic, and no software fixes a habit of silence.
