# Risk vs uncertainty

**Risk is when you know the odds, like a dice roll. Uncertainty is when you don't even know the odds, so you can't price it.**

1921 · Frank Knight · [Original book](https://www.econlib.org/library/Knight/knRUP.html) · Feeds 1 axis

## The idea

Some unknowns come with odds you can look up. A fair die lands on six one time in six, and an insurer knows roughly how many forty year olds die each year. You can't say what will happen next time, but you can say how often it happens over many tries. In his 1921 book *Risk, Uncertainty and Profit*, Frank Knight called this risk.

Other unknowns have no odds at all. There is no long record of similar cases, and the future may not look like the past. Nobody could tell you the chance that a brand new product will sell, or that a new disease will spread. Knight called this uncertainty, and today it is often called Knightian uncertainty. His point was that risk can be insured and priced away. Uncertainty can't, so whoever takes it on earns the profit or eats the loss. The trait this sorts problems on is whether the odds are knowable.

## What goes wrong

The common mistake is to treat uncertainty as if it were risk. You give a number to something that has no number, and the number makes you feel safe. Models built on past data then look precise while being wrong about the thing that matters. The reverse mistake also happens. People refuse to use odds even when good records exist, and trust a hunch over an actuarial table.

## What to do instead

The better response is to ask whether a long record of like cases exists. If it does, use the odds. If it doesn't, drop the false precision and keep enough slack to survive being wrong.

## Examples

### Life insurance

A life insurer can't know when any one customer will die. It can know, from centuries of records, how many of a million customers will die this year. That is risk in Knight's sense, and it is why the business can run on thin margins and steady premiums.

### The 2008 crash

Banks managed their exposure with models that took past price moves as a guide to future ones. Many of those models treated a nationwide fall in US house prices as close to impossible, because the recent decades of data they used held no such fall. When it did happen, the losses were far outside anything the models allowed. The banks had treated uncertainty as risk.

### Starting a business

Someone opening the first electric scooter rental in a city has no table of odds to consult. They can't buy insurance against nobody wanting scooters. If they are right they keep the profit, and if they are wrong they lose their stake. This is the bet Knight said entrepreneurs are paid to make.

## Further reading

- [Risk, Uncertainty and Profit](https://www.econlib.org/library/Knight/knRUP.html), Frank Knight, Houghton Mifflin, 1921. The full book, free from the Library of Economics and Liberty.
- [Knightian uncertainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knightian_uncertainty) on Wikipedia.
- [Knight on risk and uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1086/261461), Stephen LeRoy and Larry Singell, *Journal of Political Economy*, 1987. Argues that Knight's uncertainty meant cases where insurance markets break down, not a lack of odds in people's heads.
- [Radical Uncertainty](https://wwnorton.com/books/9781324004776), John Kay and Mervyn King, W. W. Norton, 2020. A book length case that most big decisions in business and policy face Knight's kind of uncertainty.
