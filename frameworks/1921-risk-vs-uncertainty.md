# Risk vs uncertainty

Some unknowns come with odds you can look up. A fair die lands on six one time in six, and an insurer knows roughly how many forty year olds die each year. You can't say what will happen next time, but you can say how often it happens over many tries. In his 1921 book *Risk, Uncertainty and Profit*, Frank Knight called this risk.

Other unknowns have no odds at all. There is no long record of similar cases, and the future may not look like the past. Nobody could tell you the chance that a brand new product will sell, or that a new disease will spread. Knight called this uncertainty, and today it is often called Knightian uncertainty. His point was that risk can be insured and priced away. Uncertainty can't, so whoever takes it on earns the profit or eats the loss.

The trait this sorts problems on is whether the odds are knowable. The common mistake is to treat uncertainty as if it were risk. You give a number to something that has no number, and the number makes you feel safe. Models built on past data then look precise while being wrong about the thing that matters. The reverse mistake also happens. People refuse to use odds even when good records exist, and trust a hunch over an actuarial table.

## Examples

**Life insurance.** A life insurer can't know when any one customer will die. It can know, from centuries of records, how many of a million customers will die this year. That is risk in Knight's sense, and it is why the business can run on thin margins and steady premiums.

**The 2008 crash.** Banks managed their exposure with models that took past price moves as a guide to future ones. Many of those models treated a nationwide fall in US house prices as close to impossible, because the recent decades of data they used held no such fall. When it did happen, the losses were far outside anything the models allowed. The banks had treated uncertainty as risk.

**Starting a business.** Someone opening the first electric scooter rental in a city has no table of odds to consult. They can't buy insurance against nobody wanting scooters. If they are right they keep the profit, and if they are wrong they lose their stake. This is the bet Knight said entrepreneurs are paid to make.
