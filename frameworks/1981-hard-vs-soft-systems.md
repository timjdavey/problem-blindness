# Hard vs soft systems

**In a hard problem people agree on the goal. In a soft problem people see the situation differently.**

1981 · Peter Checkland · [Original book](https://books.google.com/books/about/Systems_Thinking_Systems_Practice.html?id=icXaAAAAMAAJ) · Feeds 1 axis

## The idea

Some problems arrive with the goal already agreed. Everyone wants the bridge to carry the load, so the work is to find the cheapest safe design. Other problems arrive as a feeling that something is wrong, and each person involved tells a different story about what. The British systems thinker Peter Checkland called the first kind hard and the second kind soft. The trait he sorts on is whether people share a view of what the problem is.

Checkland came to this from experience. He spent years as a manager at ICI before joining Lancaster University, where his team tried to apply systems engineering to messy business problems. The methods kept failing. They assumed a clear goal and asked how to reach it, but in a real firm the goal was the thing people argued about. Out of that work came soft systems methodology. It starts by mapping how each group sees the situation, and only then asks what change they could all live with.

## What goes wrong

The danger runs mostly one way. Treat a soft problem as hard and you pick one group's view of the goal, then work hard to reach it. The answer can be clever and still be rejected, because half the people never agreed it was the right question. Treating a hard problem as soft wastes time, but we think it rarely does as much harm.

## What to do instead

The better response is to check first whether people agree on what the problem is. If they don't, map how each group sees it before anyone starts on a fix.

## Examples

### Apollo

In May 1961 John F. Kennedy set the goal of landing a man on the Moon and bringing him back before the decade was out. Nobody at NASA had to debate what success meant. Apollo was extremely difficult, yet in Checkland's terms it was a hard problem. Hard here means the goal is agreed, not that the work is easy. The goal was fixed, so all the effort could go into reaching it.

### A&E waiting times

England's hospitals are judged on how many patients are admitted, transferred or discharged within four hours of arriving. To managers the problem is the target. To nurses it is too few staff, and to consultants it is beds blocked by patients who can't be sent home. A hard fix like a new triage rota can hit the number while moving the queue to another ward. A soft approach starts by getting these groups to agree on what the problem actually is.

### The family holiday

One person wants a beach, another wants a city and the children want a pool. Comparing flight prices is a hard method, and it gets nowhere. The real question is what the trip is for, and until that is settled no price is the right one.
