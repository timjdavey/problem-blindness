# Presented vs discovered problems

At school almost every problem arrives ready made. Someone else has chosen the question and knows the answer, so the job is to find it. Most problems outside school aren't like that. Nobody tells you what the question is, and the hard part is working out what is worth solving. Jacob Getzels called the first kind presented problems and the second kind discovered problems.

The trait this framework sorts on is who did the framing. With a presented problem the framing is done and you only need to solve it. With a discovered problem the framing is most of the work, and a good frame can make the solution easy. Getzels and Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi found that the time and care people put into framing predicts how original their answers are. They called this problem finding.

The mistake is to treat a discovered problem as if it had been presented. You grab the first framing that comes to mind, often the one someone handed you, and start solving. The work can be skilled and still miss, because the question was wrong. Experts are prone to this, since their training is in solving problems of a known shape.

This framework feeds the [Well vs ill-structured](../axes/well-vs-ill-structured.md) axis.

## Examples

**The art students.** In the early 1960s Getzels and Csikszentmihalyi gave 31 students at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago a table of objects and asked each to arrange some and draw them. They watched how many objects each student handled and whether they changed the set up as the drawing went on. Students who explored longer before settling made work that judges rated more original. Years later, the same habit predicted which students were still working as successful artists. Their 1976 book reported the results.

**Langley and the Wrights.** Samuel Langley ran the Smithsonian, and in 1898 the US War Department gave him $50,000 to build a flying machine. He took the problem as it came: build a craft with enough power to stay up. His Aerodrome fell into the Potomac twice in the autumn of 1903. The Wright brothers had framed the problem differently. They decided the real gap was control, so they spent years learning to balance gliders before they added an engine. On 17 December 1903 they flew at Kitty Hawk.

**A set essay and a dissertation.** A set essay question tells you what to argue about. A dissertation leaves you to find the question, and most students find that much harder than the writing. The ones who spend their first weeks narrowing the question usually write the better work.
