# Complex problem solving

Dietrich Dörner, a German psychologist, wanted to know how people cope with problems that have many parts joined together, so he built some. His team wrote computer simulations of towns and regions and put people in charge of them. Players could set taxes, build and farm. Then they watched the results over many simulated years. Most did badly, and they did badly in the same ways.

The trait these problems share is that many variables are linked, some are hidden and effects arrive late. Running an economy or managing a forest is like this. Dörner found people treated such problems as a set of simple ones. They picked the most visible issue, fixed it hard and ignored the side effects. When results were slow to show, they pushed harder, then overcorrected when the delayed effects landed all at once. Many gave up on the hard goals and retreated into small tasks they felt good at.

The better players behaved differently. They asked more questions before acting and checked what their moves had done. They also thought about the side effects of each move, not only the main effect. That is the better response: gather facts first, act in small steps and check for side effects before the next move. Dörner published the work in 1989 as Die Logik des Misslingens, later translated as The Logic of Failure.

## Examples

**Tanaland.** One simulation gave players control of Tanaland, a made-up poor region in Africa with farmers and herders. Players dug wells, fought disease and improved the farming. At first it worked, as more children survived and the herds grew. Then the larger herds stripped the grassland and the wells drained the ground water, and famine followed. The players had fixed each problem on its own and missed the links between water, grass and people.

**The cold store.** In another study people had to keep a cold store at a set temperature using a dial. The temperature only responded after a delay. Many turned the dial, saw nothing happen and turned it further. Then the temperature swung past the target, so they swung the dial back too hard. Anyone who has fought a shower with a slow hot tap has done the same thing.
