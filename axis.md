# Axes for sorting problems

Each axis is a scale with two ends, and any problem sits somewhere along it. They fall into three groups. The first is about the world you act on. The second is about what you can know about it. The third is about the decision itself, what it needs and what it costs to get wrong.

## The world

These describe the system, whoever is looking at it.

| Name | Description | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterministic vs statistical | Do the same inputs always give the same result, or only a stable spread of results? A sum is deterministic. Which boiler breaks down this winter is statistical, though the rate across many boilers is steady. | [Simplicity, disorganised complexity and organised complexity](frameworks/1948-simplicity-disorganised-and-organised-complexity.md), [Clocks and clouds](frameworks/1966-clocks-and-clouds.md), [Task environment properties](frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md) |
| Thin vs fat tails | When results are statistical, how extreme can a single one get? Heights are thin tailed, so the average tells you a lot. Floods, pandemics and startup returns are fat tailed, so one outlier can outweigh everything else and past averages mislead you. | [Mediocristan vs Extremistan](frameworks/2007-mediocristan-vs-extremistan.md), [Fourth quadrant](frameworks/2008-fourth-quadrant.md) |
| Smooth vs rugged | If you change the inputs a little, do the outputs change only a little? On a smooth problem rules of thumb work and the details matter less. On a rugged one a tiny change can flip the answer, so you have to get the details right. | [Convex vs non-convex optimisation](frameworks/1970-convex-vs-non-convex-optimisation.md), [Rugged fitness landscapes](frameworks/1987-rugged-fitness-landscapes.md) |
| Long vs short predictability horizon | How far ahead does a forecast hold before small errors blow up? A cup pushed off a table is easy to predict. The weather two weeks out is not, even though the physics is simple. This is called chaos. | [Sensitive dependence (chaos)](frameworks/1963-sensitive-dependence-chaos.md), [Clocks and clouds](frameworks/1966-clocks-and-clouds.md) |
| Transparent vs opaque causes | Can you trace the chain from cause to effect? A lever is transparent. A large codebase follows rules, yet no one person can hold the whole chain in their head. | [Decision strategies matrix](frameworks/1959-decision-strategies-matrix.md), [Normal accidents](frameworks/1984-normal-accidents.md), [Detail vs dynamic complexity](frameworks/1990-detail-vs-dynamic-complexity.md), [Stacey matrix](frameworks/1996-stacey-matrix.md), [Cynefin](frameworks/1999-cynefin.md) |
| Stationary vs drifting | Do the lessons you learn today still hold next year? Gravity stays put. Music tastes, fashion and slang keep moving, so old data goes stale. | [VUCA](frameworks/1987-vuca.md), [Task characteristics of expertise](frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md), [Conditions for intuitive expertise](frameworks/2009-conditions-for-intuitive-expertise.md) |
| Inert vs reflexive | Does looking at the system, or acting on your forecast, change it? A rock doesn't care if you measure it. Publish a forecast that a bank will fail and people pull their money out, which makes it fail. | [Keynesian beauty contest](frameworks/1936-keynesian-beauty-contest.md), [Self-fulfilling prophecy](frameworks/1948-self-fulfilling-prophecy.md), [Goodhart's law](frameworks/1975-goodharts-law.md), [Campbell's law](frameworks/1976-campbells-law.md), [Lucas critique](frameworks/1976-lucas-critique.md), [Reflexivity](frameworks/1987-reflexivity.md) |
| Neutral vs adversarial | Is someone working against you? Rain doesn't try to beat your umbrella. Fraudsters, hackers and rival traders learn your methods and change their play to beat them. | [Zero-sum vs non-zero-sum games](frameworks/1944-zero-sum-vs-non-zero-sum-games.md), [Task environment properties](frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md) |
| Decomposable vs holistic | Can you split the problem into parts, solve each one and put them back together? A car engine mostly can. A family, an ecosystem or a company culture can't, because the parts only make sense together. | [Simplicity, disorganised complexity and organised complexity](frameworks/1948-simplicity-disorganised-and-organised-complexity.md), [Near decomposability](frameworks/1962-near-decomposability.md), [Messes](frameworks/1974-messes.md), [Complex problem solving](frameworks/1989-complex-problem-solving.md) |
| Loose vs tight coupling | When one part fails, how fast does it spread? In a loose system there is slack and time to step in. In a tight one, like a power grid or a nuclear plant, one fault can cascade before anyone reacts. | [Normal accidents](frameworks/1984-normal-accidents.md) |

## The knower

These describe what you can find out. The same problem can sit at different points for different people.

| Name | Description | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known vs unknown odds | Do you know the chances, or only that things are uncertain? A dice roll has known odds. Whether a new technology takes off does not, and pretending it does gives you false confidence. | [Risk vs uncertainty](frameworks/1921-risk-vs-uncertainty.md), [Ambiguity aversion](frameworks/1961-ambiguity-aversion.md), [Four levels of uncertainty](frameworks/1997-four-levels-of-uncertainty.md), [Levels of uncertainty](frameworks/2003-levels-of-uncertainty.md), [Deep uncertainty](frameworks/2003-deep-uncertainty.md), [Incertitude matrix](frameworks/2007-incertitude-matrix.md), [Radical uncertainty](frameworks/2020-radical-uncertainty.md) |
| Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty | Could more information shrink your doubt, or is it pure chance? If it's reducible you should go and gather data. If it's irreducible you should hedge instead. | [Uncertainty vs equivocality](frameworks/1986-uncertainty-vs-equivocality.md), [Puzzles vs mysteries](frameworks/2001-puzzles-vs-mysteries.md) |
| Full vs partial observability | Can you see everything that matters? In chess you see the whole board. In poker the other hands are hidden, and in medicine you often see only symptoms, not the cause. | [Johari window](frameworks/1955-johari-window.md), [Complex problem solving](frameworks/1989-complex-problem-solving.md), [Task environment properties](frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md), [Known and unknown unknowns](frameworks/2002-known-and-unknown-unknowns.md) |
| Kind vs wicked feedback | Do you find out quickly and honestly whether you were right? A chess player gets fast and clear feedback, so skill builds. A fund manager waits years for noisy results, so experience can teach the wrong lesson. | [Task characteristics of expertise](frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md), [Kind vs wicked learning environments](frameworks/2001-kind-vs-wicked-learning-environments.md), [Conditions for intuitive expertise](frameworks/2009-conditions-for-intuitive-expertise.md) |
| Plentiful vs scarce data | Do you have enough cases to learn from? Card fraud happens millions of times a year. A nuclear accident or a once in a century flood gives you almost nothing to go on. | [Clinical vs statistical prediction](frameworks/1954-clinical-vs-statistical-prediction.md), [Task characteristics of expertise](frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md), [Inside vs outside view](frameworks/1993-inside-vs-outside-view.md) |
| Explicit vs tacit know-how | Can the expertise be written down? A tax rule can. | Familiar vs novel to the solver | Has the solver seen this kind of problem before? A doctor sees a common cold every day but a rare disease once in a career. For an AI model the question is whether the problem sits inside its training data, which decides if it is reasoning or recalling. | [Programmed vs non-programmed decisions](frameworks/1960-programmed-vs-non-programmed-decisions.md), [Routine vs non-routine technology](frameworks/1967-routine-vs-non-routine-technology.md), [Skill, rule and knowledge-based behaviour](frameworks/1983-skill-rule-and-knowledge-based-behaviour.md) |

## The decision

These describe what you are trying to achieve and what happens if you get it wrong.

| Name | Description | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Well-posed vs ill-posed | Is the goal clear and do you know when you are done? A maths exam question is well posed. "Fix homelessness" is not, so working out what the problem actually is becomes part of the job. | [Well vs ill-structured problems](frameworks/1964-well-vs-ill-structured-problems.md), [Tame vs wicked problems](frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md), [Presented vs discovered problems](frameworks/1976-presented-vs-discovered-problems.md), [Single vs double-loop learning](frameworks/1978-single-vs-double-loop-learning.md), [High ground vs swampy lowlands](frameworks/1983-high-ground-vs-swampy-lowlands.md), [Problem typology](frameworks/2000-problem-typology.md) |
| Single vs multiple objectives | Is there one thing to get right, or several that pull against each other? The fastest route home is one objective. A new car trades price against safety, comfort and running costs, so there are many good answers, not one. | [Problem typology](frameworks/2000-problem-typology.md) |
| Shared vs contested values | Does everyone agree what a good result looks like? A bridge should stay up, and nobody argues. Where to build the bridge, and whose homes it passes, depends on whose interests count. | [Decision strategies matrix](frameworks/1959-decision-strategies-matrix.md), [Tame vs wicked problems](frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md), [Hard vs soft systems](frameworks/1981-hard-vs-soft-systems.md), [Post-normal science](frameworks/1993-post-normal-science.md), [Technical vs adaptive challenges](frameworks/1994-technical-vs-adaptive-challenges.md), [Stacey matrix](frameworks/1996-stacey-matrix.md), [Simple, complex and wicked](frameworks/2000-simple-complex-and-wicked.md), [Deep uncertainty](frameworks/2003-deep-uncertainty.md) |
| Satisficing vs optimising | Is good enough fine, or does the last bit matter? Picking a lunch spot is satisficing. Setting the dose of a strong drug, or tuning a racing car, needs the best answer you can find. | Novel |
| Easy vs hard to verify | Can you check an answer cheaply, even if finding it was hard? A solved sudoku takes seconds to check. A business strategy or a diagnosis may take years to prove right or wrong. | [Well vs ill-structured problems](frameworks/1964-well-vs-ill-structured-problems.md), [NP problems](frameworks/1971-np-problems.md), [Tame vs wicked problems](frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md) |
| Tractable vs intractable search | How many options do you have to search through? Chess is fully visible, fixed and rule bound, yet the number of possible games is so vast that you can't check them all. | [NP problems](frameworks/1971-np-problems.md) |
| One-shot vs sequential | Is it a single choice, or a chain where each step shapes the next? Choosing a paint colour is one shot. Running a project or treating a long illness is a series of moves, and it's hard to tell which early move caused the final result. | [Multi-armed bandit](frameworks/1933-multi-armed-bandit.md), [Exploration vs exploitation](frameworks/1991-exploration-vs-exploitation.md), [Task environment properties](frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md) |
| Reversible vs irreversible | Can you undo it if it goes wrong? Trying a new recipe is a two way door. | Survivable vs ruinous | Could one bad outcome take you out of the game for good? If so, the average result over many tries is useless, because you won't get many tries. ## Grain and horizon

Where a problem sits on most axes depends on how closely you look and over what time. One boiler breaking down is random, but a fleet of ten thousand breaks down at a steady rate. Weather is chaotic two weeks out, while climate is steady over decades. So before you place a problem on any axis, say what scale and what timescale you mean, or the same problem will get tagged two different ways.
