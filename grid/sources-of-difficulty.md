# Sources of difficulty

**Seven plain questions about what makes a problem hard, crossed with the five groups, to see which kinds of hard each group is missing.**

## The columns

Dörner's simulated towns were hard because their variables were many, linked, hidden and moving. VUCA names volatility and complexity among its four. Weaver and Senge split complexity into size, links and chance. The columns take those sources one at a time. Two of them, other people and undoing, come from frameworks outside the four checked, since none of the four asks them.

| Column | The question | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Varies | Does the same setup give a different result each time? | Weaver's disorganised complexity, where parts act at random and only the average can be known ([Simplicity, disorganised and organised complexity](../frameworks/1948-simplicity-disorganised-and-organised-complexity.md)) |
| Moves | Does it change while you work on it, or between one use and the next? | VUCA's volatility ([VUCA](../frameworks/1987-vuca.md)) and Dörner's towns, which kept changing year by year ([Complex problem solving](../frameworks/1989-complex-problem-solving.md)) |
| Interacts | Do the parts push on each other, so you can't take them one at a time? | Dörner's linked variables, VUCA's complexity and Weaver's organised complexity |
| Others | Are other people or agents part of the problem? | None of the four. Russell and Norvig's question of whether other agents are acting ([Task environment properties](../frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md)) |
| See | Can you observe the state, the causes and the results? | Dörner's hidden states and Senge's cause and effect sitting far apart ([Detail vs dynamic complexity](../frameworks/1990-detail-vs-dynamic-complexity.md)) |
| Big | Is it too big to search, hold in your head or handle at once? | Senge's detail complexity, Weaver's too many parts to track and Dörner's many variables |
| Undo | Can you take a move back or try again? | None of the four. Rittel and Webber's one-shot operation, which leaves marks that can't be undone ([Tame vs wicked problems](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md)) |

## What it shows

### Empty cells

Fourteen of the 35 cells are empty. Four are real leads. The rest are asked by an axis in another cell or were tested before.

| Cell | Candidate axis | The question | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Environment, See | Observable vs hidden state | Does the system show its state to anyone who looks? | Already an axis: [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](../axes/reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md) asks whether hidden facts can be found |
| Environment, Big | Few vs many parts | Does the system have too many parts to track? | Already an axis: many parts only hurt when their combinations must be searched, which [Tractable vs intractable options](../axes/tractable-vs-intractable-options.md) asks |
| Information, Varies | Clean vs noisy readings | Does the same thing read differently each time you measure it? | [Already tested](../log.md#2026-10-27) |
| Information, Moves | Fresh vs stale facts | Do the facts you gathered go out of date before you act on them? | Already an axis: [Turn-based vs real-time](../axes/turn-based-vs-real-time.md), since a world that moves while you work makes every reading age |
| Information, Interacts | Separate vs entangled clues | Does each clue mean something alone, or only in light of the others? | Already an axis: [Reductionist vs holistic](../axes/reductionist-vs-holistic.md), applied to evidence |
| Information, Big | Digestible vs overwhelming information | Is there more to read than anyone can sift, so the signal hides in the pile? | Untested |
| Actions, Varies | Precise vs shaky execution | Does the same move come out the same way each time you make it? | Untested |
| Actions, See | Visible vs hidden effects of moves | Can you see what your move did? | Already an axis: [Transparent vs opaque causes](../axes/transparent-vs-opaque-causes.md) |
| Performance, Varies | Steady vs noisy grading | Does the same work get a different score each time it is judged? | [Already tested](../log.md#2026-10-22) |
| Performance, Big | Few vs many criteria | Are there too many things to score for anyone to hold the whole scorecard? | Already an axis: [Single objective vs trade-offs](../axes/single-objective-vs-trade-offs.md) |
| Agent, Varies | Consistent vs noisy judgement | Would the same solver, or two solvers, give the same answer to the same case? | Untested |
| Agent, Moves | Steady vs drifting solver | Does the task run long enough that the solver changes, through fatigue, forgetting or new staff? | Untested |
| Agent, Interacts | Separable vs joined-up expertise | Can each part go to a specialist, or must one head hold all the skills? | Already an axis: [Reductionist vs holistic](../axes/reductionist-vs-holistic.md) |
| Agent, Undo | One shot vs many attempts | Does the solver get several goes before an answer counts? | Already an axis: [Reversible vs irreversible](../axes/reversible-vs-irreversible.md) |

Two of the leads sit close to existing axes. Precise vs shaky execution is near [Deterministic vs stochastic](../axes/deterministic-vs-stochastic.md), but there the world adds the noise and here your own hand does. Consistent vs noisy judgement is near [Explicit vs tacit know-how](../axes/explicit-vs-tacit-know-how.md), which says gut feel adds noise but never asks how much.

### Overfull cells

- **Environment, Varies** (4). No merge. These are the Randomness set, and each asks something different about the spread. The column is too coarse to tell them apart.
- **Environment, Moves** (4). No merge. Errors grow, rules drift, the state moves while you act and the system ages with use. Wears out joining from Stimulus hints that the Change and Stimulus sets overlap.
- **Information, See** (6). Split candidate. The column asks about three things at once: the state (Irreducible uncertainty, Unknown unknowns), the causes (Opaque causes, Observational) and the results (Unverifiable, Delayed feedback). Split See three ways and each pair gets its own cell.

### Straddles and axes with no home

Two axes straddle.

- [Inert vs reflexive](../axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md): other people react, and their reaction moves the system between forecasts.
- [Symmetric vs asymmetric information](../axes/symmetric-vs-asymmetric-information.md): the facts are hidden, and the one hiding them is another party with goals of their own.

Fourteen axes have no home. They fall into four families the columns never ask about.

- **Odds and outcomes**, VUCA's uncertainty and ambiguity: Knightian uncertainty, Unforeseeable outcomes and Ill-structured.
- **The shape of the goal**: Divergent, Optimising, Unequal error costs, No-win and Exacting landscape.
- **How much you have to go on**: Scarce data, Low base rate, Invented options and Single bet.
- **The solver's know-how**: Tacit know-how and Novel to the solver.

### Axes outside their group

- [Tractable vs intractable options](../axes/tractable-vs-intractable-options.md) lands in Actions, not Environment. The hard end is too many options to try, and options are the Options set's question. It sits in Structure because Go's size comes from the board. This looks like a small fault in the group, not the grid.

Every other placed axis stays in its own row, so the groups hold up well against this list.

## Verdict

It fails as a full test. Fourteen axes have no home, which is more than a quarter of the set. The straddles are few, only two, and the empty cells read as real questions. Four of them are untested leads and none is nonsense. But the seven sources describe a hard world. They don't describe a hard goal, a hard bet or a hard solver. Dörner's list had many goals and VUCA had uncertainty and ambiguity, and the columns dropped both.

What it sees that the groups don't is families that run across them. The Others column gathers seven axes from all five groups, from Adversarial in the world to Accountable choice in the solver. The Undo column gathers four from four groups: Tipping point, Live only, Irreversible and Ruinous. The sets keep each of these apart, so nobody checking a problem asks "who else is in it?" or "can I get another go?" once across the whole list. The See column also shows that the Evidence set is mostly one question with three parts: can you see the state, the causes or the results.
