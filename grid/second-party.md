# Second party

**Each column asks who sits on the other side of the problem, the thing whose behaviour makes the hard end hard.**

## The columns

| Column | The question | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody | Is it hard even with no one else involved and nothing moving, just you against a fixed problem? | The single agent, static end of [Task environment properties](../frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md), where a crossword waits for you and nobody else plays |
| Nature | Does the difficulty come from chance or a world that moves without intent? | The stochastic and dynamic ends of [Task environment properties](../frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md) |
| Allies | Does it come from people you need or work with, whose goals overlap yours but not fully? | The cooperative multi agent case in [Task environment properties](../frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md), the partner in a [non-zero-sum game](../frameworks/1944-zero-sum-vs-non-zero-sum-games.md) and the agent in [Agency costs](../frameworks/1976-agency-costs.md) |
| Rivals | Does it come from people who gain from your loss or study your rule to beat it? | The opponent in a [zero-sum game](../frameworks/1944-zero-sum-vs-non-zero-sum-games.md) and the competitive multi agent case in [Task environment properties](../frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md) |
| Future self | Does it come from the you who lives with the result later and may want something else? | Strotz's planner who breaks tomorrow the plan made today, in [Time inconsistency](../frameworks/1955-time-inconsistency.md) |

## What it shows

### Empty cells

| Cell | Candidate axis | The question | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Environment, Allies | Solo vs coordinated | Does the result depend on several people's moves fitting together, when none of them can set the others' moves? | Untested |
| Environment, Future self | None | How the world behaves can't come from your own later wants. | Not a question |
| Information, Future self | Known vs unknown future wants | Can you know now how you will value the result once you live with it? | [Already tested](../CONTRIBUTING.md#is-it-a-new-axis), as Fixed vs moving goal |
| Actions, Rivals | Open vs countered moves | Does a rival read each move and answer it, so your moves must stay hard to read? | Already an axis: [Neutral vs adversarial](../axes/neutral-vs-adversarial.md) already says to keep part of the rule hidden or changing |
| Performance, Rivals | Absolute vs relative standard | Is good set by a fixed bar, or only by beating what rivals manage? | Untested |
| Agent, Nature | Steady vs noisy judgement | Does the task need a solver who makes the same call on the same case every time, when people and models drift by chance? | Untested |
| Agent, Rivals | Private vs copied edge | Does the solver's know-how stop paying once rivals learn it too? | [Already tested](../log.md#2026-10-27), as Crowded vs empty field |
| Agent, Future self | Firm vs wavering will | Will the solver later want to break the plan they make today? | [Already tested](../log.md#2026-10-27), as Pays now vs pays later |

Solo vs coordinated sits close to [Needs others to change](../axes/yours-to-fix-vs-needs-others-to-change.md), which asks who holds the levers but not whether moves must match. Absolute vs relative standard may fail the third test, since [Optimising](../axes/satisficing-vs-optimising.md) and [Adversarial](../axes/neutral-vs-adversarial.md) together cover much of it. Steady vs noisy judgement is near the failed noisy readings idea, but that put the noise in the world's signal, not in the solver.

### Straddles and axes with no home

- **Symmetric vs asymmetric information** straddles Allies and Rivals. The lemon seller and the agent both want the deal, but on the hidden fact their gain is your loss.
- **Single objective vs trade-offs** straddles Nobody and Future self. One person's goals clash, and the page names now against later as the most common clash.
- **Inert vs reflexive** has no home. Savers in a bank run act with intent, so they are not nature, but they neither work with you nor aim at you. The [Keynesian beauty contest](../frameworks/1936-keynesian-beauty-contest.md) describes this party, the crowd whose guesses set the answer, and no column holds it.

### Axes outside their group

None. The columns ask who causes the difficulty, which cuts across the groups rather than along them. Nothing in a second party question pulls an axis into a different row, so this grid can't test the groups at all.

## Verdict

It half passes. Forty nine of 50 axes find a home and only two straddle, but eight of the 17 filled cells are overfull. Nobody and nature take 41 axes between them, while rivals and your future self hold one primary axis each. Of the eight empty cells, three are untested leads, three were already tested, one is already an axis and one is not a question.

What it sees that the groups don't is how few axes have a second person in them. Only eight of 50 have allies, rivals or your future self as their main cell. The rest would be just as hard on a desert island, facing a fixed puzzle or the weather. The axis left over points at a sixth party the columns miss, a crowd that reacts to what you put out. Two of the three new leads also put other people at the centre, people you must coordinate with and rivals who set the bar.
