# Update Log

## 2026-10-22

* **Creation**: Added a Stimulus set to the world group, for what a push does to the system, whether it comes from you or from outside. [Loose vs tight coupling](/axes/loose-vs-tight-coupling.md) moved into it from Structure, since it asks how a shock travels.
* **Creation**: Added the [Self-righting vs tipping](/axes/self-righting-vs-tipping.md) axis, which asks whether a system goes back to where it was after a push, stays where it was pushed or flips into a new state. It names its middle, malleable, since a Word document neither rights itself nor tips. It differs from [Reversible vs irreversible](/axes/reversible-vs-irreversible.md), which is about your undo button rather than what the system does by itself. A felled forest can't be un-felled, yet it grows back.
* **Creation**: Added the [Antifragile vs wears out](/axes/antifragile-vs-wears-out.md) axis, which asks whether many pushes leave the system stronger, the same or worn down. Self-righting asks where a system settles after one push, and this asks how it has changed after many. A bridge rights itself after every lorry and still wears out.
* **Creation**: Added the [Resilience and stability](/frameworks/1973-resilience-and-stability.md) and [Critical transitions](/frameworks/2001-critical-transitions.md) frameworks.
* **Update**: [Fragile, robust and antifragile](/frameworks/2012-fragile-robust-and-antifragile.md) now separates robust from resilient. Fragile, robust and resilient were considered as an axis of their own and went in as the ends and middle of the two Stimulus axes instead.
* **Update**: Pulled apart three axes that kept blurring together, because each one answered "is there one right answer?" and borrowed the others' words. Each now asks one question. [Single objective vs trade-offs](/axes/single-objective-vs-trade-offs.md) asks how many parts the goal has. [Convergent vs divergent](/axes/convergent-vs-divergent.md) asks whether two good answers can be blended, so many good phones that trade battery life for size now count as convergent. [Shared vs contested values](/axes/shared-vs-contested-values.md) asks who owns the goal, and its action now says to publish whose weights the model uses, leaving the trade offs themselves to the first axis. Merging them was considered and turned down, since they call for clashing moves: blend, never blend, and settle who picks. The link between Convergent and Single objective is now independent, and [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md#three-reasons-for-many-good-answers) sets out the split. The METR tags used the old wording, and the taggers put Convergent and Single objective in the same band on 167 of 252 units.
* **Update**: Renamed axes whose names were vague or easy to mix up, and wrote every end label in full. Long vs short predictability horizon is now [Stable vs chaotic](/axes/stable-vs-chaotic.md), since chaos is the field's own word and Lyapunov stability is its opposite. Known vs unknown odds is now [Risk vs Knightian uncertainty](/axes/risk-vs-knightian-uncertainty.md), after Knight's own split. Test vs watch named two things you do, so it is now [Interventional vs observational](/axes/interventional-vs-observational.md), which names the evidence the world lets you get. Single vs multiple objectives is now [Single objective vs trade-offs](/axes/single-objective-vs-trade-offs.md), since several goals that agree act as one. Easy vs hard to verify clashed with every axis's easy and hard ends, so it is now [Verifiable vs unverifiable](/axes/verifiable-vs-unverifiable.md). Complete vs incomplete score sounded like tractable vs intractable search, so it is now [Direct vs proxy measure](/axes/direct-vs-proxy-measure.md). Ends vs never-ending is now [Finite vs ongoing](/axes/finite-vs-ongoing.md).
* **Update**: Every end label now makes sense on its own, so "Fat" became "Fat tails" and "None" became "No skin in the game". [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md#naming-an-axis) adds this as a rule for naming an axis, along with using the field's own term and naming the problem rather than the response.
* **Update**: [Transparent vs opaque causes](/axes/transparent-vs-opaque-causes.md) now uses a trained neural network as its opaque example. A large codebase was a poor one, since a stack trace lets you follow it.
* **Creation**: Added the [Ends vs never-ending](/axes/finite-vs-ongoing.md) axis, which asks whether there is a point where the problem is solved or only a level you have to keep up. Rittel and Webber listed having no stopping rule as one trait of a wicked problem, but no axis held it until now. Diabetes care passes the corner test, since it is well structured and stationary and still never ends.
* **Creation**: Added the [Winnable vs damage limitation](/axes/winnable-vs-damage-limitation.md) axis, which asks whether even the best outcome on offer is a loss. It differs from [Survivable vs ruinous](/axes/survivable-vs-ruinous.md), which asks how bad the worst case is rather than how good the best case is.
* **Creation**: Added the [Prospect theory](/frameworks/1979-prospect-theory.md), [Finite and infinite games](/frameworks/1986-finite-and-infinite-games.md) and [Outcome bias](/frameworks/1988-outcome-bias.md) frameworks.
* **Creation**: Added the [Still vs moving](/axes/still-vs-moving.md) axis, which asks whether the world waits while you act or keeps changing even if you do nothing. Russell and Norvig's static vs dynamic was the one property of the [Task environment properties](/frameworks/1995-task-environment-properties.md) that fed no axis, and METR's "dynamic environment" factor had no match. Tennis passes the corner test, since its rules never drift and a ball in flight is easy to predict, yet every rally moves.
* **Creation**: Added the [Bounded vs open-ended skill](/axes/bounded-vs-open-ended-skill.md) axis, which asks whether the know-how a task needs has a ceiling that everyone competent reaches. Riding a bike is tacit yet bounded, and research taste has no ceiling and no search. It is closest to [Complete vs incomplete score](/axes/direct-vs-proxy-measure.md) and [Familiar vs novel to the solver](/axes/familiar-vs-novel-to-the-solver.md), but neither covers a test that fills up or a sub-skill that stops predicting once it is mastered.
* **Creation**: Added the [Open vs closed skills](/frameworks/1957-open-vs-closed-skills.md) framework, from E. C. Poulton's 1957 paper in Psychological Bulletin, and the [Constrained vs unconstrained skills](/frameworks/2005-constrained-vs-unconstrained-skills.md) framework, from Scott Paris's 2005 paper in Reading Research Quarterly.
* **Update**: Drew a firmer line between [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) and [Neutral vs adversarial](/axes/neutral-vs-adversarial.md). Reflexive used to cover anything people did in response to your model, so every adversarial problem was reflexive too, and both told you to hide or change your score. Now they split on whether anyone is aiming at your rule. Reflexive is a crowd believing or following your output, like a bank run, with no adversary in it. Adversarial is anyone whose goals differ from yours hunting for the cases your rule gets wrong, and that includes teachers drilling the test as well as fraudsters. Goodhart's law and Campbell's law now feed Neutral vs adversarial, and their links to verifying, the score and accountability moved with them. Both corners pass. A bank run is reflexive but neutral, and a chess opponent is adversarial but inert.
* **Update**: Split Kind vs wicked feedback up. Hogarth's wicked bundles several ways experience can mislead you, and most already had their own axis. Only delay had no home, so the axis is now [Immediate vs delayed feedback](/axes/immediate-vs-delayed-feedback.md). A ten year drug trial passes the corner test, since it is fair, stable and inert yet slow to grade. The other parts went in as notes. A noisy grade went to [Deterministic vs statistical](/axes/deterministic-vs-statistical.md), the road not taken to [Test vs watch](/axes/interventional-vs-observational.md), the typhoid doctor to [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) and practice that doesn't match the real task to [Stationary vs drifting](/axes/stationary-vs-drifting.md). [Kind vs wicked learning environments](/frameworks/2001-kind-vs-wicked-learning-environments.md) now lists the parts and feeds all five. Links from Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty, Reversible vs irreversible, Test vs watch and Winnable vs damage limitation were about the other parts, so they were dropped.

## 2026-10-15

* **Creation**: Added the [Algorithm selection problem](/frameworks/1976-algorithm-selection-problem.md) framework, from John Rice's 1976 chapter in *Advances in Computers*. It asks how to pick a method from features of the case you can measure first. It feeds [Clustered vs scattered](/axes/clustered-vs-scattered.md), and [No free lunch](/frameworks/1997-no-free-lunch.md) now links to it.

## 2026-10-14

* **Update**: Renamed four axes. Well-posed vs ill-posed is now [Well vs ill-structured](/axes/well-vs-ill-structured.md), since "well-posed" means something else in maths and Simon's term is the source. One-shot vs sequential is now [Standalone vs sequential](/axes/standalone-vs-sequential.md), because Russell and Norvig's easy end is many choices that stand alone, not one choice. Complete vs incomplete goal is now [Complete vs incomplete score](/axes/direct-vs-proxy-measure.md), since it is the score that leaves things out. Decomposable vs holistic is now [Decomposable vs tangled](/axes/decomposable-vs-tangled.md).
* **Update**: [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md) now uses Davey's own test, which asks whether repeated runs draw from the same spread. A die is coherent again. The train trip stays as a contrast, since every week of trips has the same two humps. The camouflage and pilot stories moved to [Clustered vs scattered](/axes/clustered-vs-scattered.md).
* **Update**: Fixed smaller faults found in a review of every axis. NP-complete problems are no longer called the hardest searches of all. [Small vs large world](/axes/small-vs-large-world.md) now opens with its procedure test, and [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) covers scores and actions as well as forecasts. Links to [Satisficing vs optimising](/axes/satisficing-vs-optimising.md) now give Simon's real reason, and [Long vs short predictability horizon](/axes/stable-vs-chaotic.md) now says how it differs from drift. Every axis names its subgroup, every link goes both ways and each citation list matches its sources.
* **Update**: Reviewed every framework for accuracy and clarity. [Incoherence](/frameworks/2024-incoherence.md) now says its measure compares whole runs, so a die is coherent by that measure but fails the wider test in [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md). The train example moved to the axis only, and a study of 100 BERT copies took its place. [Ergodicity](/frameworks/2016-ergodicity.md) no longer slides into ruin, and [Puzzles vs mysteries](/frameworks/2001-puzzles-vs-mysteries.md) names Gladwell's drift from Treverton. [Simple, complex and wicked](/frameworks/2000-simple-complex-and-wicked.md) warns that "complex" means three different things across the frameworks.
* **Update**: Fixed factual slips in about fifteen frameworks. Fat tails are now about whether one case can outweigh the rest, not whether extremes are capped. NICE's limit is per year of healthy life, and Goodhart's broad money target went in 1986. Schelling, Porter, Rosenthal and Jacobson now get credit for their examples, and the Cohesion climate case is marked as a thought experiment.
* **Update**: Cut repeated stories. Kodak is told in full once, under [Single vs double-loop learning](/frameworks/1978-single-vs-double-loop-learning.md), and Pruitt-Igoe once, under [High ground vs swampy lowlands](/frameworks/1983-high-ground-vs-swampy-lowlands.md). [Tame vs wicked problems](/frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md) now uses Boston's Central Artery. Every framework now says the trait it sorts on and the better response, and links to the axes that cite it.
* **Update**: Added three tests a new axis must pass to the [README](/README.md) and [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md#when-is-it-a-new-axis). Six ideas for new axes failed them, so they went in as notes instead. Delays went to [Kind vs wicked feedback](/axes/immediate-vs-delayed-feedback.md), self-defeating forecasts to [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) and loops that feed on themselves to [Thin vs fat tails](/axes/thin-vs-fat-tails.md). Varied cases went to [Plentiful vs scarce data](/axes/plentiful-vs-scarce-data.md), moving goals to [Complete vs incomplete score](/axes/direct-vs-proxy-measure.md) and now against later to [Single vs multiple objectives](/axes/single-objective-vs-trade-offs.md). The law of requisite variety and the garbage can model are now cited by an axis.
* **Update**: [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md) now asks whether the average stands for a typical outcome, to match its one line description. A die now counts as incoherent, since no face shows 3.5.
* **Update**: Fixed smaller faults found by checking every axis against the three tests. [Well-posed](/axes/well-vs-ill-structured.md) now needs known moves, so "cure this cancer" is ill-posed by its own test. [Easy vs hard to verify](/axes/verifiable-vs-unverifiable.md) is now about the cost of checking before you act, not how long the world takes to grade you. Five Correlated with tags were corrected.
* **Update**: Split each of the three axis groups into smaller subgroups. The world now has Randomness, Change, Response and Structure. The knower has Uncertainty, Evidence and Know-how. The decision has Goal, Options, Stakes and People. Every axis has a `subgroup` field, and the [axes index](/axes/index.md) and [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md) list them.
* **Creation**: Added the [Spread vs single bet](/axes/spread-vs-single-bet.md) axis, which asks whether you can split your stake across several options at once or must take one course of action. It differs from [Reversible vs irreversible](/axes/reversible-vs-irreversible.md), which spreads your tries across time rather than across options.
* **Creation**: Added the [Portfolio selection](/frameworks/1952-portfolio-selection.md) framework, from Harry Markowitz's 1952 paper in the Journal of Finance.

* **Update**: Split the divergent end of [Convergent vs divergent](/axes/convergent-vs-divergent.md) into answers that differ in degree and answers that differ in kind. Blending two good answers works for the first and fails for the second, which is the answer side of [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md). A separate Dial vs fork axis was considered and folded in here.

* **Update**: Separated [Small vs large world](/axes/small-vs-large-world.md) from [Tractable vs intractable search](/axes/tractable-vs-intractable-search.md). A small world is one where a fixed procedure generates the outcomes, so you can sample them however many there are. Intractable search only applies once the options are known. Go, with about 10¹⁷⁰ legal positions, replaces chess as the intractable example, and each axis now links to the other.
