# Update Log

## 2026-10-15

* **Creation**: Added the [Algorithm selection problem](/frameworks/1976-algorithm-selection-problem.md) framework, from John Rice's 1976 chapter in *Advances in Computers*. It asks how to pick a method from features of the case you can measure first. It feeds [Clustered vs scattered](/axes/clustered-vs-scattered.md), and [No free lunch](/frameworks/1997-no-free-lunch.md) now links to it.

## 2026-10-14

* **Update**: Renamed four axes. Well-posed vs ill-posed is now [Well vs ill-structured](/axes/well-vs-ill-structured.md), since "well-posed" means something else in maths and Simon's term is the source. One-shot vs sequential is now [Standalone vs sequential](/axes/standalone-vs-sequential.md), because Russell and Norvig's easy end is many choices that stand alone, not one choice. Complete vs incomplete goal is now [Complete vs incomplete score](/axes/complete-vs-incomplete-score.md), since it is the score that leaves things out. Decomposable vs holistic is now [Decomposable vs tangled](/axes/decomposable-vs-tangled.md).
* **Update**: [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md) now uses Davey's own test, which asks whether repeated runs draw from the same spread. A die is coherent again. The train trip stays as a contrast, since every week of trips has the same two humps. The camouflage and pilot stories moved to [Clustered vs scattered](/axes/clustered-vs-scattered.md).
* **Update**: Fixed smaller faults found in a review of every axis. NP-complete problems are no longer called the hardest searches of all. [Small vs large world](/axes/small-vs-large-world.md) now opens with its procedure test, and [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) covers scores and actions as well as forecasts. Links to [Satisficing vs optimising](/axes/satisficing-vs-optimising.md) now give Simon's real reason, and [Long vs short predictability horizon](/axes/long-vs-short-predictability-horizon.md) now says how it differs from drift. Every axis names its subgroup, every link goes both ways and each citation list matches its sources.
* **Update**: Reviewed every framework for accuracy and clarity. [Incoherence](/frameworks/2024-incoherence.md) now says its measure compares whole runs, so a die is coherent by that measure but fails the wider test in [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md). The train example moved to the axis only, and a study of 100 BERT copies took its place. [Ergodicity](/frameworks/2016-ergodicity.md) no longer slides into ruin, and [Puzzles vs mysteries](/frameworks/2001-puzzles-vs-mysteries.md) names Gladwell's drift from Treverton. [Simple, complex and wicked](/frameworks/2000-simple-complex-and-wicked.md) warns that "complex" means three different things across the frameworks.
* **Update**: Fixed factual slips in about fifteen frameworks. Fat tails are now about whether one case can outweigh the rest, not whether extremes are capped. NICE's limit is per year of healthy life, and Goodhart's broad money target went in 1986. Schelling, Porter, Rosenthal and Jacobson now get credit for their examples, and the Cohesion climate case is marked as a thought experiment.
* **Update**: Cut repeated stories. Kodak is told in full once, under [Single vs double-loop learning](/frameworks/1978-single-vs-double-loop-learning.md), and Pruitt-Igoe once, under [High ground vs swampy lowlands](/frameworks/1983-high-ground-vs-swampy-lowlands.md). [Tame vs wicked problems](/frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md) now uses Boston's Central Artery. Every framework now says the trait it sorts on and the better response, and links to the axes that cite it.
* **Update**: Added three tests a new axis must pass to the [README](/README.md) and [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md#when-is-it-a-new-axis). Six ideas for new axes failed them, so they went in as notes instead. Delays went to [Kind vs wicked feedback](/axes/kind-vs-wicked-feedback.md), self-defeating forecasts to [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) and loops that feed on themselves to [Thin vs fat tails](/axes/thin-vs-fat-tails.md). Varied cases went to [Plentiful vs scarce data](/axes/plentiful-vs-scarce-data.md), moving goals to [Complete vs incomplete score](/axes/complete-vs-incomplete-score.md) and now against later to [Single vs multiple objectives](/axes/single-vs-multiple-objectives.md). The law of requisite variety and the garbage can model are now cited by an axis.
* **Update**: [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md) now asks whether the average stands for a typical outcome, to match its one line description. A die now counts as incoherent, since no face shows 3.5.
* **Update**: Fixed smaller faults found by checking every axis against the three tests. [Well-posed](/axes/well-vs-ill-structured.md) now needs known moves, so "cure this cancer" is ill-posed by its own test. [Easy vs hard to verify](/axes/easy-vs-hard-to-verify.md) is now about the cost of checking before you act, not how long the world takes to grade you. Five Correlated with tags were corrected.
* **Update**: Split each of the three axis groups into smaller subgroups. The world now has Randomness, Change, Response and Structure. The knower has Uncertainty, Evidence and Know-how. The decision has Goal, Options, Stakes and People. Every axis has a `subgroup` field, and the [axes index](/axes/index.md) and [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md) list them.
* **Creation**: Added the [Spread vs single bet](/axes/spread-vs-single-bet.md) axis, which asks whether you can split your stake across several options at once or must take one course of action. It differs from [Reversible vs irreversible](/axes/reversible-vs-irreversible.md), which spreads your tries across time rather than across options.
* **Creation**: Added the [Portfolio selection](/frameworks/1952-portfolio-selection.md) framework, from Harry Markowitz's 1952 paper in the Journal of Finance.
* **Update**: Added a barbell example to [Fragile, robust and antifragile](/frameworks/2012-fragile-robust-and-antifragile.md).
* **Update**: Split the divergent end of [Convergent vs divergent](/axes/convergent-vs-divergent.md) into answers that differ in degree and answers that differ in kind. Blending two good answers works for the first and fails for the second, which is the answer side of [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md). A separate Dial vs fork axis was considered and folded in here.
* **Update**: Folded Full vs partial observability into [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](/axes/reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md). A hidden fact is the main kind of reducible doubt, and the two axes gave the same advice.
* **Update**: Separated [Small vs large world](/axes/small-vs-large-world.md) from [Tractable vs intractable search](/axes/tractable-vs-intractable-search.md). A small world is one where a fixed procedure generates the outcomes, so you can sample them however many there are. Intractable search only applies once the options are known. Go, with about 10¹⁷⁰ legal positions, replaces chess as the intractable example, and each axis now links to the other.
* **Update**: Rewrote every axis description to describe each end in one line with a short example, in place of a single long question. The [axes index](/axes/index.md) now lists both ends under each axis.
* **Update**: Reframed [Smooth vs rugged](/axes/smooth-vs-rugged.md) around how far a small change in the inputs can move the result, following Tim Davey. Local peaks are now one result of that, and the axis cites the [Flat maximum](/frameworks/1973-flat-maximum.md).
* **Creation**: Added the [Coherent vs incoherent](/axes/coherent-vs-incoherent.md) axis, which asks whether repeated runs agree well enough for the average to stand for a typical run.
* **Creation**: Added the [Incoherence](/frameworks/2024-incoherence.md) framework, from Tim Davey's 2024 paper in Entropy.
* **Creation**: Added the [Clustered vs scattered](/axes/clustered-vs-scattered.md) axis, which asks whether runs that disagree still fall into a few clear groups.
* **Creation**: Added the [Cohesion](/frameworks/2023-cohesion.md) framework, from Tim Davey's 2023 paper in Entropy.
* **Update**: Moved the project to the [Open Knowledge Format](https://okf.md) 0.2. Every framework and axis now has `type`, `title`, `description` and `sources` front matter.
* **Creation**: Added the root [index](/index.md), the [axes index](/axes/index.md), the [frameworks index](/frameworks/index.md) and the [Reading the axes](/axes/reading-the-axes.md) guide, which replace the folder READMEs.
* **Update**: Folded Commensurable vs incommensurable values into [Single vs multiple objectives](/axes/single-vs-multiple-objectives.md). It only applies when there are several objectives, and its main action matched setting a floor for each one.
* **Update**: Folded Convex vs concave payoff into [Thin vs fat tails](/axes/thin-vs-fat-tails.md). Its actions were already covered by fat tails, ruin and irreversibility, so only the point about changing your exposure moved across.
* **Update**: Removed Discrete vs continuous. Its hard end isn't harder, since whole number problems are often the harder search, and its actions were already under [Easy vs hard to verify](/axes/easy-vs-hard-to-verify.md). Moravec's paradox now sits in [Explicit vs tacit know-how](/axes/explicit-vs-tacit-know-how.md).
