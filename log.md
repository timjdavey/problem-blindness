# Update Log

## 2026-10-27

* **Update**: Renamed Known vs unknown unknowns to [Foreseeable vs unforeseeable outcomes](/axes/foreseeable-vs-unforeseeable-outcomes.md). Yesterday's name was wrong. Rumsfeld's terms are about the facts that go into a choice, and whether you know which ones you're missing. The page asks about what comes out, and whether you can list every way things might turn out. Foreseeable is the word the law uses for whether a reasonable person could have seen a harm coming, and it doesn't sound like Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty. The page now cites Stirling's [Incertitude matrix](/frameworks/2007-incertitude-matrix.md), whose ignorance box is the same idea, and Rumsfeld moves to a new axis about inputs.
* **Update**: [Foreseeable vs unforeseeable outcomes](/axes/foreseeable-vs-unforeseeable-outcomes.md) now covers unintended consequences, the side effects of your own action. We tested Intended vs unintended consequences as a separate axis and it failed the third [new axis test](/CONTRIBUTING.md#is-it-a-new-axis). Unforeseeable outcomes, [opaque causes](/axes/transparent-vs-opaque-causes.md), a [reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) system and a [proxy measure](/axes/direct-vs-proxy-measure.md) already flag the same problems and give the same moves. Whether side effects can happen at all is linked to [Reductionist vs holistic](/axes/reductionist-vs-holistic.md) and [Buffered vs cascading](/axes/buffered-vs-cascading.md).
* **Creation**: Added the [Unanticipated consequences](/frameworks/1936-unanticipated-consequences.md) framework, from Robert Merton's 1936 paper in the *American Sociological Review*. It backs the new side effects action on Foreseeable vs unforeseeable outcomes.

Reflexive used to cover anything people did in response to your model, so every adversarial problem was reflexive too, and both told you to hide or change your score. Now they split on whether anyone is aiming at your rule. Reflexive is a crowd believing or following your output, like a bank run, with no adversary in it. Adversarial is anyone whose goals differ from yours hunting for the cases your rule gets wrong, and that includes teachers drilling the test as well as fraudsters. Goodhart's law and Campbell's law now feed Neutral vs adversarial, and their links to verifying, the score and accountability moved with them. Both corners pass. A bank run is reflexive but neutral, and a chess opponent is adversarial but inert.
* **Update**: Split Kind vs wicked feedback up. Hogarth's wicked bundles several ways experience can mislead you, and most already had their own axis. Only delay had no home, so the axis is now [Immediate vs delayed feedback](/axes/immediate-vs-delayed-feedback.md). A ten year drug trial passes the corner test, since it is fair, stable and inert yet slow to grade. The other parts went in as notes. A noisy grade went to [Deterministic vs statistical](/axes/deterministic-vs-stochastic.md), the road not taken to [Test vs watch](/axes/interventional-vs-observational.md), the typhoid doctor to [Inert vs reflexive](/axes/inert-vs-reflexive.md) and practice that doesn't match the real task to [Durable vs perishable lessons](/axes/durable-vs-perishable-lessons.md). Links from Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty, Reversible vs irreversible, Test vs watch and Winnable vs damage limitation were about the other parts, so they were dropped.
