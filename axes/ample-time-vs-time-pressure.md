# Ample time vs time pressure

An axis in the [Control](index.md#control) set of the [actions](index.md#actions) group.

Do you have time to gather facts and think before things change, or must you act in seconds or minutes? A pension plan gives you months. A pilot with a failed engine has seconds.

## Actions

**Ample time**: Take your time.
- Use your most careful method, even if it is slow. An actuary can run the numbers twice and a model can reason for longer. The usual case for settling on the first option that will do is that search costs time, and here time is cheap. [1]
- Ask others what the problem really is before you act. A boss who calls every project urgent skips this slow work, so the hard questions never get asked. [5]

**Time pressure**: Decide the playbook in advance.
- Work out answers and fallbacks ahead of time. In the moment, matching the case to a stored rule is fast and reasoning from scratch is slow. That is why a pilot with a failed engine reaches for a checklist. [2]
- Check the plan fits the time you will really have. Flight 1549's checklist for losing both engines assumed a jet at cruising height. At under 3,000 feet the crew could run only part of it. [2]
- Take the first plan that will work rather than compare options. Klein found that expert fire commanders pictured the first plan that came to mind and used it if it seemed to work. That pays off because they have years of practice in a setting they know. [1]
- Act first to make things stable, and look for causes later. Waiting for the evidence to settle is itself a decision, and often a costly one. [3, 4]
- Check the deadline is real. A leader who likes giving orders has every reason to call a problem a crisis, since a crisis leaves little room for debate. [5]
- When the clock runs for years, take small steps now that are hard to reverse. Each actor has a reason to wait, so pick steps that build their own support. The UK's carbon budgets set a path that later governments found hard to drop. [6]

## Correlated with

- [Loose vs tight coupling](loose-vs-tight-coupling.md) (logic). Perrow's slack is a buffer in the process, while this axis is about time to decide, but the two meet. When a tight system fails it hands you a decision with no time ([Normal accidents](../frameworks/1984-normal-accidents.md)).
- [Explicit vs tacit know-how](explicit-vs-tacit-know-how.md) (study). Hammond's [cognitive continuum](../frameworks/1987-cognitive-continuum.md) puts tasks with little time at the intuitive end, and Klein's fire lieutenant got out on a gut call ([Conditions for intuitive expertise](../frameworks/2009-conditions-for-intuitive-expertise.md)).
- [Satisficing vs optimising](satisficing-vs-optimising.md) (tension). With no time to search, you have to settle for good enough. Klein's fire commanders took the first plan that would work rather than compare options ([Satisficing](../frameworks/1956-satisficing.md)).
- [Tractable vs intractable search](tractable-vs-intractable-search.md) (tension). A search too big to finish, done against the clock, is the worst case.
- [Shared vs contested values](shared-vs-contested-values.md) (bundled). [Post-normal science](../frameworks/1993-post-normal-science.md) and [super wicked problems](../frameworks/2007-super-wicked-problems.md) both join a clock to a fight over values. Grint warns that leaders sometimes call a problem a crisis to justify giving orders ([Tame, wicked and critical](../frameworks/2005-tame-wicked-and-critical.md)), so check that the deadline is real.
- [Yours to fix vs needs others to change](yours-to-fix-vs-needs-others-to-change.md) (bundled). [Super wicked problems](../frameworks/2007-super-wicked-problems.md) join a clock to a world with nobody in charge, so each actor has a reason to wait while time runs out.
- [Winnable vs no-win](winnable-vs-no-win.md) (study). Crises are often no-win, which is part of why Grint's critical problems call for command ([Tame, wicked and critical](../frameworks/2005-tame-wicked-and-critical.md)). Flight 1549 had minutes and no good outcome. But a falling market gives you time to think and still only offers losses.
- [Static vs dynamic](static-vs-dynamic.md) (tension). This axis says decide the playbook in advance, while a dynamic world says a plan made in advance goes stale. The way out is to plan responses to cues, not a fixed sequence of moves. The two still come apart, since a bomb with a timer sits still and gives you minutes.
