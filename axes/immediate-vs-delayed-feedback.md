# Immediate vs delayed feedback

An axis in the [Evidence](index.md#evidence) set of the [information](index.md#information) group.

How long after you act do you find out whether you were right? A darts player sees where each dart lands a second after throwing it, and adjusts the next one. Someone planting an apple orchard waits years for the first real crop, so a working life gives them a few dozen tries at most. Slow feedback means few tries to learn from. By the time the result comes in, the world may have moved on and you may have forgotten why you chose what you did.

Robin Hogarth put quick feedback at the heart of what he called a [kind learning environment](../frameworks/2001-kind-vs-wicked-learning-environments.md). His wicked environments go wrong in other ways too, and each of those has its own axis. A noisy grade lets luck pass for skill ([Deterministic vs stochastic](deterministic-vs-stochastic.md)). You may never see how the road not taken turned out ([Interventional vs observational](interventional-vs-observational.md)). Your own action can cause the result you take as proof ([Inert vs reflexive](inert-vs-reflexive.md)). Practice may not look like the real task ([Rehearsable vs live only](rehearsable-vs-live-only.md)), or the world may have moved on since you learnt ([Durable vs perishable lessons](durable-vs-perishable-lessons.md)). This axis is only about how long the grade takes. Hogarth's wicked is also not the wicked of [wicked problems](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md), which is about problems with no clear statement or end.

Delay comes apart from all of these. A drug trial can be fair, stable and blind to its own forecasts, and still take ten years to say how many patients survived.

## Actions

**Immediate feedback**: Learn from results.
- Adjust after every result. Grades come in fast, so you can keep up with what you get wrong, like a cook tasting as they go. [4]
- Change things in small steps and watch what each one does. Small moves show you their effect, and here you don't wait long to see it. [2]
- Trust experience, as long as the grade was honest too. An anaesthetist sees each decision play out within minutes, so years in the job build real skill. [3, 5]

**Delayed feedback**: Find an early signal, and check that it tracks the real one.
- Use a signal you can measure soon, then test that it moves with the outcome you care about. After a heart attack, extra heartbeats warn of sudden death. Two drugs calmed them, yet the CAST trial reported in 1989 that the drugs more than doubled deaths. [6]
- Judge your method on the long term result once it arrives, not only the early signal. From 1935 the US Forest Service aimed to have every fire under control by the next morning. For decades that looked like success, while dead wood piled up and later fires burned hotter. [2]
- Don't react to the latest reading while earlier moves are still working through. In the beer game a single rise in demand sets orders swinging up the chain. Beer takes weeks to arrive, so players order more while earlier orders are still on their way. [2]
- Write down what you knew and why you chose, at the time. When the result arrives years later, you can learn from the reasons instead of judging the choice by how it turned out. [1]
- Don't take years of experience as proof of skill. A radiologist often never learns whether a reading was right, so long practice can leave them sure and still wrong. [3, 5]
- Lean on other people's results when your own tries are few. Base rates stand in for feedback you won't live to see. [4]

## Correlated with

- [Stable vs chaotic](stable-vs-chaotic.md) (study). Weather forecasters are graded the next day and are well calibrated ([Task characteristics of expertise](../frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md)). Tetlock's pundits made calls years ahead, which took years to grade, and did little better than chance ([Conditions for intuitive expertise](../frameworks/2009-conditions-for-intuitive-expertise.md)).
- [Transparent vs opaque causes](transparent-vs-opaque-causes.md) and [Standalone vs sequential](standalone-vs-sequential.md) (study). In Senge's [beer game](../frameworks/1990-detail-vs-dynamic-complexity.md) delays hide which move caused which result.
- [Plentiful vs scarce data](plentiful-vs-scarce-data.md) (logic). Slow feedback means few graded tries. [Shanteau](../frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md) found experts got good where the same decision came round often.
- [Durable vs perishable lessons](durable-vs-perishable-lessons.md) (study). [Shanteau](../frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md) and [Kahneman and Klein](../frameworks/2009-conditions-for-intuitive-expertise.md) found experience turns into skill only where the setting is stable and feedback comes soon. Delay makes drift worse, since the world may have moved by the time the grade arrives.
- [Verifiable vs unverifiable](verifiable-vs-unverifiable.md) (logic). Feedback is the world checking your answer for you, so an answer that is hard to check often gives slow feedback. They still differ. Verifying is checking one answer before you act on it. Feedback is the world grading you after you act, over many tries. A sudoku is easy to check but teaches you little about the next one. You can't check tomorrow's weather forecast before you act on it, yet the next day grades it clearly.
- [Skin in the game vs none](skin-in-the-game-vs-none.md) (logic). If the cost of a mistake lands on someone else, news of it reaches the decider late or never.
- [Clustered vs scattered runs](clustered-vs-scattered-runs.md) (logic). Clear groups give early signs of which path you are on. Scattered runs give signs that point nowhere in particular.
- [Curable vs chronic](curable-vs-chronic.md) (independent). Ongoing means no final grade, not no grade. A glucose meter tells a diabetic within minutes whether a dose was right.
- [Rehearsable vs live only](rehearsable-vs-live-only.md) (independent). A rehearsal grades you before you go, and feedback grades you after. A canary deploy grades you in minutes but had no faithful rehearsal. A pension plan can be backtested against a century of markets and still takes decades to grade.
