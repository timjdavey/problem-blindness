# Curable vs chronic

An axis in the [Goal](index.md#goal) set of the [decision](index.md#the-decision) group.

Is there a point where the problem is solved and you can stop, or only a level you have to keep up for good? Smallpox ended. The last natural case was in Somalia in 1977, and in 1980 the World Health Assembly declared it gone. Malaria looked like it might go the same way. Sri Lanka began spraying homes with DDT in 1946, and by 1963 it recorded just 17 cases. Most of the spraying stopped that year, and by 1969 there were over half a million cases again. Malaria was treated as a job that could be finished, so it came back as soon as the work stopped.

Problems never end for three reasons. The target can move, because the world drifts or reacts to what you do. The problem can grow back, as weeds, rust and bugs do even in a world that stays the same. Or there can always be more to gain, as in science or a business that wants to grow. Rittel and Webber listed having no stopping rule as one trait of a wicked problem, and said the traits should be dealt with one at a time ([Tame vs wicked problems](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md)). This axis is that trait on its own. James Carse called a game played to keep playing, rather than to win, an infinite game ([Finite and infinite games](../frameworks/1986-finite-and-infinite-games.md)).

Watch the grain. An ongoing problem is usually a chain of tasks that each end. A sprint ends but the product doesn't, and a harvest ends but the farm doesn't. Tag the problem at the level where the owner and the budget sit, as [Grain and horizon](reading-the-axes.md#grain-and-horizon) says.

## Actions

**Curable**: Define the finish and stop when you reach it.
- Write the test for done before you start. The model can then work towards it and stop.
- Fund it as a project. Once it is done the people can move on.

**Chronic**: Plan for the level you hold, not the finish.
- Define success as a level held over time, such as uptime or cases a year. Measure the trend, not a single pass.
- Budget for running costs before you build. Retraining, monitoring and fixing a model cost money every year it runs.
- Expect the software around the model to need changes forever. Meir Lehman's first law of software evolution, from 1974, says a program used in the real world must keep changing or grow less useful.
- Give it an owner who stays. A team that ships and leaves hands over a problem nobody holds.
- Test agents on long runs. A benchmark task ends when the agent says it is done, so it never checks whether the agent can keep something working.
- Be wary of anyone declaring victory, person or model. Stopping the work is how malaria came back.

## Correlated with

- [Well vs ill-structured](well-vs-ill-structured.md) (bundled). Simon's well-structured problems have a clear end, and [Rittel and Webber](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md) and [Grint](../frameworks/2005-tame-wicked-and-critical.md) both say a wicked problem has none. But the two come apart. Keeping a diabetic's blood sugar in range is well structured and never ends. "Cure this cancer" is ill-structured and would end.
- [Stationary vs drifting](stationary-vs-drifting.md) (logic). A drifting world is one reason a problem never ends, since every answer goes stale. But weeds grow back in a garden where nothing about the world has changed.
- [Inert vs reflexive](inert-vs-reflexive.md) (logic). When people react to your fix, the fix becomes part of the next problem, so there is no point where you are done.
- [Satisficing vs optimising](satisficing-vs-optimising.md) (independent). Optimising can feel endless, since each extra bit of quality pays. Yet a drug dose gets tuned once and stays tuned. A lawn only needs to look fine, but it needs mowing every week.
- [Standalone vs sequential](standalone-vs-sequential.md) (logic). Each round of work on an ongoing problem starts from where the last one left things. But a chain of choices can still end. Treating a long illness is sequential and stops when the patient is well.
- [Verifiable vs unverifiable](verifiable-vs-unverifiable.md) (logic). With no end there is no final answer to check, only the level so far.
- [Immediate vs delayed feedback](immediate-vs-delayed-feedback.md) (independent). Ongoing means no final grade, not no grade. A glucose meter tells a diabetic within minutes whether a dose was right.
- [Winnable vs no-win](winnable-vs-no-win.md) (independent). Carse's finite game both ends and can be won, but the two traits come apart. A crash landing is over in minutes and can't be won. A farm never finishes, yet it can have a good harvest every year.
