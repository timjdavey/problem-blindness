# Shared vs contested values

An axis in the [Goal](index.md#goal) set of the [performance](index.md#performance) group.

Do all the people affected rank the possible results the same way, or do they want different things, with nobody who can settle it alone? A bridge should stay up, and nobody argues. Where to build the bridge, and whose homes it passes, depends on whose interests count.

This axis asks who owns the goal, not how many parts it has. [Single objective vs trade-offs](single-objective-vs-trade-offs.md) says to lay out the trade offs and let the decision maker pick. That assumes there is one decision maker whose pick counts. Here there isn't. Two towns that each want the bridge have one objective each, a bridge near them. Their goals are simple, but they rank the sites in opposite orders.

## Actions

**Shared values**: Go straight to the technical work.
- Let experts build it and judge it. Everyone agrees what good looks like.
- Keep the review inside the team. There is no outside group whose view you are missing.

**Contested values**: Settle whose values count before you build, and do it in the open.
- Bring the affected groups in early. Their objections are cheaper to hear before launch than after.
- Say whose weights the model uses and who chose them. If people can see whose side the weights take, they can argue with them.
- Let people outside the team challenge the choices. An outside review catches the side you didn't know you took.
- Don't ship one side's view as neutral fact. If nobody asks whose side a model is on, that is what it does.

## Correlated with

- [Neutral vs adversarial](neutral-vs-adversarial.md) (logic). Rivals often want different things, so the two tend to come together. But two players in a [zero-sum game](../frameworks/1944-zero-sum-vs-non-zero-sum-games.md) agree on what winning means. They each just want to be the winner. Contested values is a fight over what a good result even is.
- [Risk vs Knightian uncertainty](risk-vs-knightian-uncertainty.md) and [Ample time vs time pressure](ample-time-vs-time-pressure.md) (bundled). [Post-normal science](../frameworks/1993-post-normal-science.md) joins disputed values with uncertain facts and urgent deadlines, and [Deep uncertainty](../frameworks/2003-deep-uncertainty.md) joins them with unknown odds. [Stirling](../frameworks/2007-incertitude-matrix.md) and [Thompson and Tuden](../frameworks/1959-decision-strategies-matrix.md) keep facts and values apart, so this is a link in practice, not by definition.
- [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md) (bundled). Daft and Lengel's [equivocality](../frameworks/1986-uncertainty-vs-equivocality.md) is a clash of meanings that more data won't settle.
- [Well vs ill-structured](well-vs-ill-structured.md) (bundled). [Rittel and Webber](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md), [Checkland](../frameworks/1981-hard-vs-soft-systems.md) and [Roberts](../frameworks/2000-simple-complex-and-wicked.md) all trace ill-structured problems back to a fight over goals. But "cure this cancer" has one goal that everyone shares and is still ill-structured.
- [Single objective vs trade-offs](single-objective-vs-trade-offs.md) (logic). Once there are several goals, different people weigh them differently. Stirling calls this [ambiguity](../frameworks/2007-incertitude-matrix.md). But one person buying a car has many objectives and nobody to argue with. And it runs the other way. Two towns that each want the bridge have one objective each, yet they rank the sites in opposite orders.
- [Private vs accountable](private-vs-accountable.md) (logic). A choice that picks winners has to be defended in public, which is why [Post-normal science](../frameworks/1993-post-normal-science.md) calls for a wider group to judge it.
- [Direct vs proxy measure](direct-vs-proxy-measure.md) (logic). When people disagree on what a good result is, any one score takes a side and leaves the other wants out.
- [Yours to fix vs needs others to change](yours-to-fix-vs-needs-others-to-change.md) (bundled). [Heifetz](../frameworks/1994-technical-vs-adaptive-challenges.md) says adaptive work often means people giving up something they value. [Super wicked problems](../frameworks/2007-super-wicked-problems.md) have nobody in charge to settle whose interests count.
- [Even vs lopsided mistakes](even-vs-lopsided-mistakes.md) (logic). How much worse a miss is than a false alarm is a value judgement, so people fight over where to set the bar.
- [Convergent vs divergent](convergent-vs-divergent.md) (logic). When the options differ in kind there is no middle ground to settle on. Two towns that each want the bridge can't have half of it each, so someone has to lose. But a fork with one owner is still a fork. A best man has nobody to argue with and still can't blend a funny speech with a moving one.
- [Diversifiable vs single bet](diversifiable-vs-single-bet.md) (logic). A budget can be split between rival groups, so each gets some of what it wants. A bridge can't, so someone has to lose.
- [Winnable vs no-win](winnable-vs-no-win.md) (logic). When a loss has to land somewhere, people fight over where. But everyone on Flight 1549 wanted the same thing and still couldn't win.
