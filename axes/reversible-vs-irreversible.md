# Reversible vs irreversible

An axis in the [Control](index.md#control) set of the [actions](index.md#actions) group.

If it goes wrong, can you undo it cheaply and try again, or are you stuck with it? Trying a new recipe is a two way door. Selling the family home or cutting off a leg is a one way door.

## Actions

**Reversible**: Move fast and let whoever is doing the work act alone.
- Make small changes often and learn from what breaks. You can roll back.
- Skip the sign off, whether a junior chef or an AI agent is doing the work. A mistake costs an undo, not a disaster.

**Irreversible**: Slow down and put a second pair of eyes at the door.
- Ask for sign off before any move that can't be undone. Sending money, felling a tree and deleting records are the usual ones.
- Rehearse before you act. A surgeon talks the team through the plan and a builder pegs out the walls. Each shows what they would do, so someone else can stop it before it is real.
- Turn one way doors into two way ones where you can. A trial period, a cooling off period and a delay before a message sends all buy you an undo.
- Gate only the moves that are hard to undo. Gating everything trains people to approve without reading.

## Correlated with

- [Survivable vs ruinous](survivable-vs-ruinous.md) (by definition). All ruin is irreversible, but not every irreversible choice ruins you.
- [Loose vs tight coupling](loose-vs-tight-coupling.md) (study). In [Normal accidents](../frameworks/1984-normal-accidents.md) faults cascade into damage that can't be undone before anyone can step in.
- [Familiar vs novel to the solver](familiar-vs-novel-to-the-solver.md) (bundled). Simon's [non-programmed decisions](../frameworks/1960-programmed-vs-non-programmed-decisions.md) are new and carry high stakes.
- [Standalone vs sequential](standalone-vs-sequential.md) (independent). Standalone choices can each be final, like a stream of loans where each one is gone once paid out. A chain of edits to a document is sequential, yet every step can be undone. Rittel and Webber's "one-shot operation" means a choice that can't be undone, so it belongs here rather than on that axis.
- [Private vs accountable](private-vs-accountable.md) (logic). Bezos argues one-way doors deserve a slow process with many views ([One-way vs two-way doors](../frameworks/2015-one-way-vs-two-way-doors.md)), and that brings scrutiny.
- [Interventional vs observational](interventional-vs-observational.md) (logic). You can't run a fair test of a one way door, because there is no going back to try the other side.
- [Diversifiable vs single bet](diversifiable-vs-single-bet.md) (independent). Both are ways to avoid staking everything on one guess. This axis spreads your tries across time, and that one spreads them across options. A venture fund is spread but each cheque is gone once written, and a lunch spot is a single bet you can undo.
- [Self-righting vs tipping](self-righting-vs-tipping.md) (independent). This axis is about your undo button and that one is about what the system does by itself. Deleting a paragraph in Word is reversible, but the document never puts it back on its own. A felled forest can't be un-felled, yet it grows back.
