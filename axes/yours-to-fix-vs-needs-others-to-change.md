# Yours to fix vs needs others to change

An axis in the [decision](index.md#the-decision) group.

Do you hold the levers, or does the fix depend on other people changing what they do? A surgeon can unblock a man's arteries on his own. Whether the man lives another twenty years depends on whether he stops smoking, and no surgeon can do that for him. Climate change is harder still, because nobody is in charge and the people trying to fix it are also causing it. Ronald Heifetz calls the first kind of problem technical and the second adaptive.

## Actions

**Yours to fix**: Fix it.
- Build the tool and ship it. You hold the levers.
- Measure the model directly. If it works, the problem is solved.

**Needs others**: Design for take up, not just accuracy.
- Involve the people who must change early. A tool built for them without them tends to sit unused.
- Make the change easy. Fit the model into what people already do rather than asking them to learn a new habit.
- Measure what people do, not model scores. A perfect model nobody uses fixes nothing.
- Plan for the people who lose out. If the change costs someone something they value, expect them to push back.

[Heifetz](../frameworks/1994-technical-vs-adaptive-challenges.md) says adaptive work often means people giving up something they value. [Super wicked problems](../frameworks/2007-super-wicked-problems.md) have nobody in charge to settle whose interests count.
- [Slack vs time pressure](slack-vs-time-pressure.md) (bundled). Super wicked problems join a clock to a world with nobody in charge, so each actor has a reason to wait while time runs out.
- [Decomposable vs holistic](decomposable-vs-holistic.md) (bundled). In Ackoff's [messes](../frameworks/1974-messes.md) the fix for one department becomes the next department's problem, so nobody holds the levers for the whole.
- [Inert vs reflexive](inert-vs-reflexive.md) (logic). Both put other people inside the problem. Once a fix depends on others changing, how they react to it becomes part of the problem.
- [Neutral vs adversarial](neutral-vs-adversarial.md) (independent). A chess opponent works against you, but every move you need is yours to make. Two firms in a [non-zero-sum game](../frameworks/1944-zero-sum-vs-non-zero-sum-games.md) want the same deal and still need each other to close it.
- [Explicit vs tacit know-how](explicit-vs-tacit-know-how.md) (independent). Both say an expert can't just hand over the fix, but for different reasons. Tacit know-how is hard to put into words. In an adaptive challenge the know-how is often plain, like stop smoking, and the hard part is getting people to act on it.
