# Yours to fix vs needs others to change

An axis in the [Control](index.md#control) set of the [actions](index.md#actions) group.

Do you hold the levers, or does the fix depend on other people changing what they do? A surgeon can unblock a man's arteries on his own. Whether the man lives another twenty years depends on whether he stops smoking, and no surgeon can do that for him. Climate change is harder still, because nobody is in charge and the people trying to fix it are also causing it. Ronald Heifetz calls the first kind of problem technical and the second adaptive.

## Actions

**Yours to fix**: Fix it.
- Make the fix and put it in place. In a technical problem the work sits with whoever has the know-how, as with a plumber mending a leak. [3]
- Judge the fix by whether the problem stays fixed. If it keeps coming back, someone else has to change too. A team that keeps missing deadlines gets new tracking software, and the deadlines keep slipping. The real cause is that nobody will tell the boss a date is unrealistic. [3]

**Needs others to change**: Design for take up.
- Put the work with the people who must change. No expert can change their habits for them, and a quick technical fix only lets everyone put off the painful part. [3]
- Make the change the easy path. When a large US firm enrolled new staff in its pension plan unless they opted out, far more of them took part than when they had to sign up. Put the recycling bin next to the rubbish bin, not at the end of the street. [5]
- Measure what people do, not how good the fix looks on paper. A perfect diet plan nobody follows fixes nothing, and nor does a perfect model nobody uses. [3]
- Plan for the people who lose out. The change often asks them to give up something they value, so expect them to push back. [3]
- Look for a deal that leaves both sides better off. Two sisters who split an orange down the middle each lost half of what they could have had, since one wanted the fruit and the other the peel. [1]
- Where nobody is in charge, take small steps that build their own support. Once a policy creates jobs or habits that depend on it, it tends to stick. [4]

## Correlated with

- [Shared vs contested values](shared-vs-contested-values.md) (bundled). [Heifetz](../frameworks/1994-technical-vs-adaptive-challenges.md) says adaptive work often means people giving up something they value. [Super wicked problems](../frameworks/2007-super-wicked-problems.md) have nobody in charge to settle whose interests count.
- [Ample time vs time pressure](ample-time-vs-time-pressure.md) (bundled). Super wicked problems join a clock to a world with nobody in charge, so each actor has a reason to wait while time runs out.
- [Reductionist vs holistic](reductionist-vs-holistic.md) (bundled). In Ackoff's [messes](../frameworks/1974-messes.md) the fix for one department becomes the next department's problem, so nobody holds the levers for the whole.
- [Inert vs reflexive](inert-vs-reflexive.md) (logic). Both put other people inside the problem. Once a fix depends on others changing, how they react to it becomes part of the problem.
- [Neutral vs adversarial](neutral-vs-adversarial.md) (independent). A chess opponent works against you, but every move you need is yours to make. Two firms in a [non-zero-sum game](../frameworks/1944-zero-sum-vs-non-zero-sum-games.md) want the same deal and still need each other to close it.
- [Explicit vs tacit know-how](explicit-vs-tacit-know-how.md) (independent). Both say an expert can't just hand over the fix, but for different reasons. Tacit know-how is hard to put into words. In an adaptive challenge the know-how is often plain, like stop smoking, and the hard part is getting people to act on it.
