# Known vs unknown unknowns

An axis in the [Uncertainty](index.md#uncertainty) set of the [information](index.md#information) group.

Do you know what you don't know? Before you buy a house you know you don't know the state of the roof, so you pay for a survey. That is a known unknown. You had no reason to ask whether the neighbour's dog barks all night. That is an unknown unknown, and it is the one that makes you regret the move. Donald Rumsfeld made both names famous in 2002, though engineers had long spoken of "unk-unks".

The worst cases are the ones where experts didn't know to look. Thalidomide went on sale in West Germany in 1957 as a sleeping pill and was given to pregnant women for morning sickness. Drug tests at the time didn't check for harm to unborn babies, so nobody asked the question. Over ten thousand children were born with severe birth defects before the drug was withdrawn.

This axis is about the questions you ask, not the answers you get. Whether the answer to a question can be found is a matter for [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md). The test on thalidomide could have been run. It just wasn't on anyone's list. This axis is also about the facts that go into a choice, not the results that come out. Whether you can list every way things might turn out is a question for [Foreseeable vs unforeseeable outcomes](foreseeable-vs-unforeseeable-outcomes.md). Warren Walker and his colleagues make the same split. They ask where a doubt sits, in the inputs to a model or in its outcomes, as well as how deep it goes.

## Actions

**Known unknowns**: List the gaps and work through them.
- Write down what you know you don't know. A gap you name can be researched, hedged or insured, and one you leave unnamed can't. [3]
- Decide for each gap whether to find the answer or plan round it. That depends on whether the answer is out there, which is the question [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md) asks. [3, 4]

**Unknown unknowns**: Widen the search for what matters.
- Ask other people what you haven't asked. A blind spot can only be found by someone who stands somewhere else, so ask outsiders, newcomers and the people who will live with the result. [1]
- Imagine the plan has already failed and ask everyone why. Gary Klein found this makes it safe to raise doubts that would otherwise stay quiet. Earlier work found that picturing an event as already over helps people think of more reasons for it. [5, 6]
- Try small and watch for surprises. Some things nobody knows can only be found by doing something and seeing what turns up. [1]
- Say where the doubt sits, not just how big it is. A doubt about which forces matter needs different tools from a doubt about how big a known force will be. [2, 4]
- Prefer moves that still work whichever forces turn out to matter, and leave slack to change course. A longer list of risks won't help you, since the one that bites isn't on it. [2, 3]

## Correlated with

- [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md) (independent). This axis asks whether you know to go looking, and Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty asks whether you would find the answer once you did. All four mixes happen. A blood test is a known unknown you can settle. A rival's price cut next year is a known unknown nobody can settle. The neighbour's dog is an unknown unknown that one chat over the fence would have settled. A newspaper in 2005 that never asked how people would read news on their phones faced an unknown unknown that no survey could yet answer.
- [Foreseeable vs unforeseeable outcomes](foreseeable-vs-unforeseeable-outcomes.md) (independent). That axis asks whether you can list what might happen, and this one asks whether you know which facts you're missing. A loan is either repaid or not, so a bank can list every outcome. Its model can still miss a cause nobody thought to measure, like a local factory about to close. Apple knew every part that went into the first iPhone in 2007, yet nobody listed a taxi firm run from it, as Uber became two years later.
- [Transparent vs opaque causes](transparent-vs-opaque-causes.md) (independent). Opaque causes means you can see every part but can't follow the chain. Unknown unknowns means some parts aren't on your list at all. A neural network is opaque with nothing hidden. The neighbour's dog is a simple cause you never thought to look for.
- [Familiar vs novel to the solver](familiar-vs-novel-to-the-solver.md) (logic). Someone who has met a problem many times knows which questions to ask, so a novel problem leaves more of the gaps unnamed. Thalidomide shows experience doesn't close them all.
- [General vs local knowledge](general-vs-local-knowledge.md) (logic). The people on the spot often know which questions matter. Anyone living next door could have told you about the dog.
