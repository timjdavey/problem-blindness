# Kind vs wicked feedback

An axis in the [Evidence](index.md#evidence) set of the [knower](index.md#the-knower) group.

Do you find out soon and clearly whether you were right, or is the feedback slow, noisy or missing, so experience can teach the wrong lesson? A chess player gets fast and clear feedback, so skill builds. A fund manager waits years for noisy results, so a run of luck can pass for skill. Robin Hogarth coined these terms. His wicked is not the wicked of [wicked problems](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md), which is about problems with no clear statement or end.

## Actions

**Kind**: Learn from results.
- Retrain on outcomes. Fast, honest feedback means the labels are good.
- Trust experience. A person or model that has seen many results here has learnt something real.

**Wicked**: Don't let experience grade itself.
- Check the labels before you train on them. They may be late, noisy or missing for the cases you turned down, so the model can learn the wrong lesson.
- Get labels from outside the loop. Audits and expert review give a view the model's own record can't.
- Let a small random share through that the model would have refused. It's the only way to see how the road not taken turns out.
- Judge on long term outcomes rather than the first signal you can measure. Clicks today can hide lost customers next year.
- Don't react to the latest reading while earlier moves are still working through. In the beer game a single rise in demand sets orders swinging up the chain, because players keep ordering beer that is already on its way ([Detail vs dynamic complexity](../frameworks/1990-detail-vs-dynamic-complexity.md)).

## Correlated with

- [Plentiful vs scarce data](plentiful-vs-scarce-data.md) and [Stationary vs drifting](stationary-vs-drifting.md) (study). [Shanteau](../frameworks/1992-task-characteristics-of-expertise.md) found experts got good where decisions came often in the same form about a stable subject. [Kahneman and Klein](../frameworks/2009-conditions-for-intuitive-expertise.md) say the same of intuition you can trust.
- [Long vs short predictability horizon](long-vs-short-predictability-horizon.md) (study). Weather forecasters work inside the horizon and are well calibrated, while Tetlock's long range pundits did little better than chance.
- [Transparent vs opaque causes](transparent-vs-opaque-causes.md) and [Standalone vs sequential](standalone-vs-sequential.md) (study). In Senge's [beer game](../frameworks/1990-detail-vs-dynamic-complexity.md) delays hide which move caused which result.
- [Reducible vs irreducible uncertainty](reducible-vs-irreducible-uncertainty.md) (logic). Some hidden facts can never be found. If you never see how the road not taken turned out, as in hiring, half your feedback is missing for good ([Hogarth](../frameworks/2001-kind-vs-wicked-learning-environments.md)).
- [Easy vs hard to verify](easy-vs-hard-to-verify.md) (logic). Feedback is the world checking your answer for you, so an answer that is hard to check gives slow feedback. They still differ. Verifying is checking one answer before you act on it. Feedback is the world grading you after you act, over many tries. A sudoku is easy to check but teaches you little about the next one. You can't check tomorrow's weather forecast before you act on it, yet the next day grades it clearly.
- [Reversible vs irreversible](reversible-vs-irreversible.md) (bundled). [Rittel and Webber](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md) note you can't learn by trial and error when each try leaves a mark.
- [Test vs watch](test-vs-watch.md) (logic). Watching mixes the effect of a choice with the kind of people who made it, so it can teach the wrong lesson. Doctors read a 1991 study of nurses as proof that hormones protect the heart, and a 2002 trial showed they did harm ([Ladder of causation](../frameworks/2000-ladder-of-causation.md)).
- [Skin in the game vs none](skin-in-the-game-vs-none.md) (logic). If the cost of a mistake lands on someone else, the decider may never hear about it.
- [Clustered vs scattered](clustered-vs-scattered.md) (logic). Clear groups give early signs of which path you are on. Scattered runs give signs that point nowhere in particular.
