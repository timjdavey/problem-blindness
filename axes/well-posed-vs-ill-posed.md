# Well-posed vs ill-posed

An axis in the [decision](index.md#the-decision) group.

Is the problem clearly stated, with a known goal and a clear point where you are done, or is working out what the problem is part of the job? A maths exam question is well posed. "Fix homelessness" is not, so working out what the problem actually is becomes part of the job.

## Actions

**Well-posed**: Solve it.
- Let the model work towards the test. With a clear goal and a test for done, it doesn't need a person watching.
- Automate the check. The test for done can run on every output.

**Ill-posed**: Work out the problem before solving it.
- Draft several framings of the goal. Each one points to a different solution, so compare them before you pick.
- Write down what done looks like and agree it before building. Otherwise every review turns into an argument about the goal.
- Expect to revise the goal once the first version ships, and budget for it. Seeing a draft is often what tells people what they wanted.
- Use the model to explore, not to finish. Ask it for framings and questions rather than an answer.

## Correlated with

- [Easy vs hard to verify](easy-vs-hard-to-verify.md) (by definition). Simon's [well-structured problems](../frameworks/1964-well-vs-ill-structured-problems.md) come with a test for an answer, and [Rittel and Webber](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md) say a wicked problem has no point where it is done.
- [Shared vs contested values](shared-vs-contested-values.md) (bundled). [Rittel and Webber](../frameworks/1973-tame-vs-wicked-problems.md), [Checkland](../frameworks/1981-hard-vs-soft-systems.md) and [Roberts](../frameworks/2000-simple-complex-and-wicked.md) all say the hard part is that people don't agree what the problem is.
- [Single vs multiple objectives](single-vs-multiple-objectives.md) (bundled). Simon's house design and Jonassen's [dilemmas](../frameworks/2000-problem-typology.md) are hard to pin down because several wants pull against each other. These three axes all get bundled under "wicked", but they come apart. "Cure this cancer" has one goal that everyone shares, yet nobody can say how to get there or what to try first. Buying a car has many objectives but is well posed once you set the weights.
- [Decomposable vs holistic](decomposable-vs-holistic.md) (bundled). Ackoff's [messes](../frameworks/1974-messes.md) and Jonassen's typology put tangled problems at the ill-structured end.
- [Familiar vs novel to the solver](familiar-vs-novel-to-the-solver.md) (bundled). Simon's [non-programmed decisions](../frameworks/1960-programmed-vs-non-programmed-decisions.md) are both new and poorly structured.
- [Complete vs incomplete goal](complete-vs-incomplete-goal.md) (logic). If you can't state the goal, you can't score all of it. The reverse doesn't hold. Rating CVs looked well posed, yet Amazon's score left out fairness ([Need for interpretability](../frameworks/2017-need-for-interpretability.md)).
- [Choose vs design the options](choose-vs-design-the-options.md) (independent). "Build a bridge across this river for under £10m" is well posed, and every option still has to be drawn.
- [Convergent vs divergent](convergent-vs-divergent.md) (independent). "Write a sonnet about autumn" is well posed and has endless good answers.
