# Commensurable vs incommensurable values

An axis in the [decision](index.md#the-decision) group.

Can every want be priced in one currency? Choosing a flight trades price against time, and you can say what an hour is worth to you. A hospital budget trades money against lives, and most people refuse to put a price on a child. Alan Fiske and Philip Tetlock called these taboo trade offs.

## Actions

**Commensurable**: Price everything and add it up.
- Use a single score with weights. Every want has a price, so one number can stand for the whole.
- Let the model make the trade offs. Once the weights are agreed, it can apply them more evenly than a person.

**Incommensurable**: Set hard limits instead of weights.
- Treat sacred values as rules the model can't break, not costs it can trade. No gain elsewhere should buy a breach.
- Leave the remaining trade offs to a person who can own them. Some choices need someone to answer for them.
- Keep prices on lives and other sacred things out of the system. A loss function that puts a price on a life will be found and quoted.

## Correlated with

- [Single vs multiple objectives](single-vs-multiple-objectives.md) (by definition). The question only comes up when there is more than one objective. Keeney and Raiffa's weights assume every goal can be turned into one score ([Decisions with multiple objectives](../frameworks/1976-decisions-with-multiple-objectives.md)).
- [Shared vs contested values](shared-vs-contested-values.md) (independent). Everyone can agree a child's life is sacred and still face a budget. People can also fight over how to weigh two things that both have a price.
- [Private vs accountable](private-vs-accountable.md) (study). In 2000 Tetlock asked people to judge a hospital manager who could spend $1m on a child's liver transplant or on equipment and salaries. Choosing the equipment after long thought scored 5.71 for outrage out of 7. Saving the child at once scored 1.51. Even being seen to weigh a sacred value counts against you, so a choice you must defend in public pushes the trade off out of sight.
- [Complete vs incomplete goal](complete-vs-incomplete-goal.md) (logic). A single score has to price everything. So the sacred value is either left out or priced where nobody can see.
